## The ten things this course actually teaches

1. **VC and PE are similar but mostly different.** VC funds unprofitable
   companies through the valley of death; PE buys profitable ones with debt the
   company repays.
2. **Valuation runs backwards from the return you need** — the VC Method — because
   there's no cash flow to discount.
3. **Staging rounds is worth ~40% more to founders.** Capital isn't scarce; the
   valuation you sell it at is.
4. **Liquidation preference is downside protection that vanishes at IPO.** It
   governs mediocre exits, and it cumulates by round.
5. **Entry multiple is everything.** KKR's RJR equity grew 5x and still returned
   only ~14% IRR.
6. **Leverage amplifies whether good or bad** — and the acquired company pays.
7. **~75% of VC deals return nothing; ~47% of M&A destroys value; ~20% of large
   LBOs go bankrupt.** The base rates beat the model.
8. **Proprietary sourcing beats auctions**, because auctions bid the price up.
9. **A 10-year fund against ~8 years to IPO** explains nearly every VC behavior.
10. **You make upside money when you own something valuable.**

## The three structural insights worth more than the facts

**The fund clock.** Ten-year fund, ~8 years to IPO, ~$75M and three rounds to get
there. Everything else — exit pressure, board departures, the 90% acquisition
rate — falls out of that one constraint.

**The instrument trade.** Priced preferred, convertible note and SAFE differ on
exactly two axes: must you price the company today, and where do you sit in the
payout stack. Every other detail follows.

**Leverage vs operations.** RJR (95% debt, ~14% IRR) against Vista/Marketo (22%
debt, 3.1x in 25 months) is the same asset class run two ways — and "12 is the
new 5" explains why the second has displaced the first.
