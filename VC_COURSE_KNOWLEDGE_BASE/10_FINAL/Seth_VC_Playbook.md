# Seth's Venture Capital Playbook

**Version:** v1.0 — 2026-08-12. Built from FIN/ENTRP 4350 and 4310 as taught by
Todd Ortberg, consolidated across 13 lectures.

> A practical sequence for evaluating a venture investment. Every step is course
> material; where the course is silent, it says so rather than inventing.

---

## 1. SCREEN — is this even a venture problem?
- **Unprofitable, early stage, needs financing through the valley of death?**
  If it's profitable and stable, it's a PE problem.
- **Is the market growing far faster than ~2% US GDP?** If not, it isn't venture-scale.
- **Is there a real problem being solved that creates high growth?**

## 2. MARKET
Size · growth · validation evidence (real trial data, not assertions) ·
competitive position · **"Why now?"** — *easy to see if something is right, hard
to time it right.*

## 3. TEAM
Track record, industry expertise, operational capability. **Verify credentials** —
Coral Ventures' failure was an unverified founder background. And assume the
founder may not be CEO at exit: **only 41% still are.**

## 4. PRODUCT & MOAT
Differentiation · **underlying magic / IP — patents applied for?** · technical
debt · defensibility. Incumbent litigation is survivable; IBM sued Compaq for
eight years and lost.

## 5. ECONOMICS
**Gross margin is the strong business indicator** — margin covers a lot of sins.
Then the cash plan, which must cover **all three needs**: operating losses,
**working capital**, and **capex**. Only the first shows on the P&L.
*(Unit economics — CAC/LTV/payback — are a gap; fill from outside reading.)*

## 6. PRICE — the VC Method
```
Estimate exit revenue  ×  sector exit multiple  =  exit value
Exit value  ÷  required MOIC (4x for 40% over 4 years)  =  max post-money
```
Cross-check against **market averages by round** (NVCA, PitchBook).
**Do not DCF it** — there's often no cash flow.

## 7. STRUCTURE
Priced preferred, convertible note, or SAFE — decided by *must you price today*
and *where do you sit in the stack*.

## 8. TERMS — read them as economics vs control
**Economics:** liquidation preference (1x non-participating standard;
**preferences cumulate by round**), participation, anti-dilution (weighted
average vs full ratchet), vesting and acceleration.
**Control:** protective provisions, board, registration rights, pro rata,
information rights, ROFR, co-sale.

> **Misunderstanding the term sheet is the number one source of later
> founder/investor misalignment — especially at exit.**

## 9. MODEL THE DILUTION — including a bad outcome
Run the cap table to exit. **Then run a mediocre exit**: does the preference
stack leave the common with anything? A $38M offer against $40M raised leaves
founders roughly nothing.

And remember the staging lesson: **capital isn't scarce, the valuation you sell
it at is.**

## 10. EXIT
Who buys this? **Start with the GTM partners — they're the most likely
acquirers.** Is a CVC in the round (strategic acquirer signal, and supports a
higher price)? IPO plausible against **$75M+ / 3+ rounds / ~8 years**? Does the
timeline fit the fund's remaining life? **~90% get acquired.**

## 11. RETURN
Underwrite to **4x in 4 years or 5x in 5** (~40% IRR). Ask what has to be true —
and whether those assumptions are ordinary or heroic.

## 12. PORTFOLIO CONTEXT
**~75% of these will not return capital.** NVIDIA took $42M and became the most
valuable company in America; another deal took eight rounds and $150M and sold at
a loss. Both are normal. You are not trying to be right often — you are trying to
be **very right occasionally**.

## 13. DECIDE
Write it down. A clear pass with reasoning is as valuable as a yes.

## The ten things to carry into any room
1. **VC and PE are similar but mostly different** — and don't use the terms loosely
2. **Valuation runs backwards from the return you need**, not forwards from worth
3. **Staging rounds is worth ~40% more to founders**
4. **Liquidation preference is protection that vanishes at IPO** — it governs
   mediocre exits
5. **Entry multiple is everything** — overpaying can't be fixed operationally
6. **Leverage amplifies whether good or bad** — the company pays the debt
7. **Cost synergies are bankable; revenue synergies are a promise**
8. **~47% of M&A destroys value** — the base rate beats the model
9. **Proprietary sourcing beats auctions**, because auctions bid the price up
10. **You make upside money when you own something valuable**
