# TIER 1 — GETTABLE ~12 MONTHS AFTER RIS (no new credential required)

### 1. Alternative Investments Due Diligence Analyst
**Raymond James, St. Petersburg FL** — HQ, has the team, hires continuously.
- **Pay:** ~$75–110k start · $130–200k senior
- **Tampa:** ✅ Best-in-class — it's their headquarters
- **Politics:** Low-medium (corporate, not fund)
- **Ownership:** ❌ Salary forever
- **Coursework fit:** ✅✅ Direct — PE, private credit, hedge funds, REITs
- **Verdict:** Best *job* match. Not a wealth-building path on its own.

### 2. Wealth Management / Private Client Associate → Advisor
Raymond James, Merrill, Northwestern, or an independent RIA.
- **Pay:** $60–90k building · **$150–400k established · $500k–1M+ with a real book**
- **Tampa:** ✅✅ Florida is a top wealth market (no state income tax, retirees)
- **Politics:** ✅ Low — you own the client relationship
- **Ownership:** ✅✅ **A book of business is an asset that renews**
- **Verdict:** Only salaried-start path that *becomes* semi-passive. Slowest start.

### 3. Insurance / Annuity Company Investment Analyst
Florida has a large insurance industry; ties to `SRC-P-010`'s Global Atlantic material.
- **Pay:** $70–110k start · $130–180k senior
- **Tampa:** ✅ Good · **Politics:** ✅ Low · **Ownership:** ❌
- **Verdict:** Stable, underrated, directly uses the private credit knowledge.

### 4. Commercial Banking / Credit Analyst
- **Pay:** $65–95k start · $120–170k as a relationship manager
- **Tampa:** ✅ Good · **Politics:** Low · **Ownership:** ❌
- **Verdict:** **The single best training ground if ETA (Tier 4) is the real goal** —
  you learn to underwrite exactly the businesses you'd later buy, and you meet
  the SBA lenders.

### 5. Corporate FP&A / Treasury at a Tampa company
- **Pay:** $75–110k start · $130–180k senior · **Hours:** ✅ Best (40–45)
- **Tampa:** ✅ · **Politics:** Medium (corporate) · **Ownership:** ❌
- **Verdict:** Easiest lifestyle, lowest ceiling. Good if life outside work matters most.

# TIER 3 — PATHS YOU MAY NOT KNOW ABOUT

### 11. **Family Office Analyst** ⭐ underrated
Wealthy families running their own capital. Florida has many (tax migration).
- **Pay:** $90–160k start · $200–350k senior
- **Politics:** ✅✅ Very low — tiny teams, no carry fights
- **Around wealth:** ✅✅ Literally working for a wealthy family
- **Coursework fit:** ✅✅ They allocate to PE/VC/private credit
- **Verdict:** Quietly one of the best fits on this entire list. Hard to find —
  they hire through networks, not job boards. **Worth targeting deliberately.**

### 12. **Fund-of-Funds / LP Allocator**
You pick *funds*, not companies. The buy-side of the buy-side.
- **Pay:** $90–150k start · $200k+ senior · **Politics:** ✅ Low
- **Verdict:** All the private-markets exposure, none of the deal knife-fighting.

### 13. **RIA Ownership / Buying a Book**
Advisors retire and sell their client books — often seller-financed.
- **Verdict:** **This is ETA applied to wealth management.** Combines Tier 1 #2
  with Tier 4. Very underrated for someone already inside the industry.

### 14. **Business Broker / M&A Advisor for small businesses**
You broker the sale of $1–20M businesses.
- **Pay:** Commission — $80–250k+ · **Verdict:** You see every deal in the market
  before anyone else. **The best possible seat if you want to buy one later.**

### 15. Independent Sponsor
Do PE deals without a fund — find the deal, raise capital per deal.
- **Verdict:** Real path, but needs a network and a track record first. Later, not now.

# TIER 4 — ⭐ BUYING SMALL BUSINESSES (ETA)

**The formal name: Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition (ETA).** Stanford has
studied it for decades. This may be the best fit for what Seth actually described.

## The market
- **~12 million businesses** may change hands this decade — **$10T+ in value** —
  as boomer owners retire
- **Record 6,915 SBA-backed acquisition loans in 2025, worth $8.17B**, up from
  just over $5B in 2023

## The economics
- **SBA 7(a): as little as 10% down.** With a seller note, **$150k–$250k of your
  own capital can buy a profitable ~$2.5M business**
- Stanford data: aggregate **pre-tax IRR ~32.6%, ~5.4x on invested capital**
  across search funds; another dataset reports **35.1% IRR across 681 funds**

## ⚠️ The honest risk
- **More than half of searches fail.** Returns are **highly skewed** — top quartile
  makes outsized money, some searches are a **total loss**
- Sourcing takes **12–24 months**, then **3–6 months** to diligence and close
- **This is NOT "not much work."** You become the owner-operator of a real
  business. It is the *most* work — but it's **your** work, on **your** asset.

## Why it fits Seth's filter
| Filter | ETA |
|---|---|
| Solid money | ✅ Owner earnings + equity appreciation |
| Around wealth | ⚠️ You *become* it rather than sit near it |
| Not grinding forever | ✅ **Eventually — once you hire a GM** |
| No 10-year wait | ✅✅ **Cash flow from month one. You own it, not a carry claim** |
| Tampa | ✅✅ Buy a Florida business |
| Politics | ✅✅ **You're the owner. Nobody to backstab.** |
| Coursework | ✅✅ LBO math, EV/EBITDA, diligence, deal structure — `SRC-P-008/011/012/013` |

**This is the literal application of his professor's closing line:
*"You make upside money when you OWN SOMETHING VALUABLE."***

## The realistic on-ramp
```
Now → 1 yr    Stay at RIS. Save aggressively — the down payment is the constraint.
1 → 3 yrs     Take a job that teaches underwriting AND builds a network:
              commercial banking/credit (#4), business brokerage (#14),
              or Tampa middle-market IB (#9)
3 → 5 yrs     Search. Look at 100+ businesses, buy 1.
              Target: $1–3M purchase, $300k–800k of owner earnings
5 yrs +       Operate 3–5 years, hire a GM, then either hold for cash flow
              or sell at a higher multiple than you paid
```
