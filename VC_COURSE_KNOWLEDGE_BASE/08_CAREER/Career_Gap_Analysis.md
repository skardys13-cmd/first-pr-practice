## The real gaps

### 1. Financial modeling — **the biggest concrete gap**
The course *shows* models; it does not evidence that Seth *built* any. Nearly
every analyst role screens on this.
**Fix:** build the cap-table model and LBO model in `Portfolio_Project_Plan.md`.
This is the highest-ROI item on the list and is entirely within reach.

### 2. Transaction experience
No deal has been worked. Unfixable by study — closed only by a job, an
internship, or a very good simulated work product.
**Mitigation:** written investment memos on real companies are the accepted
substitute for candidates without deal experience.

### 3. Sourcing and network
`SRC-P-011` teaches that **proprietary deal flow is the edge**, and LPs select
managers on it. Seth has no sourcing track record and no venture network.
**Fix:** start a personal deal log now. Iowa/Midwest startups, tracked weekly.
This doubles as portfolio evidence and is credible precisely because it is local.

### 4. Startup operating experience
Frequently a hiring path into VC. Not present.
**Mitigation:** the RIS client-facing work is real professional experience; it is
just not startup experience. Don't oversell it.

### 5. Sector depth
The course's examples skew to **fiber optics, broadband, telecom infrastructure
and enterprise software** — because that is the instructor's career. That is
useful exposure but not Seth's own thesis.
**Fix:** the **Iowa insurance/retirement/private capital** angle is his and should
be developed deliberately.

### 6. Investment committee exposure
Not present and not simulable alone. Partially addressed by writing memos in IC
format.

### 7. Founder relationships
None. The most durable long-term asset in venture, and the slowest to build.
**Fix:** begin now, locally. ISU's entrepreneurship ecosystem is the natural entry.

### 8. Unit economics fluency
The course never covers CAC, LTV, payback, or retention — and these come up in
almost every early-stage conversation.
**Fix:** external reading (see `EXTERNAL_CONTEXT_Research_Canon.md` gap #4).
**This is a real hole and should be closed early.**

- **Return math** — IRR/MOIC fluency with the hurdle tables is solid
- **LBO mechanics** — strong enough to hold a PE conversation
- **M&A analysis** — accretion/dilution and the base rates are genuinely strong
- **Private markets structure** — the map of PE/VC/hedge/RE/private credit is
  more complete than most finance graduates have

transaction experience, which is the gap that study cannot close.
