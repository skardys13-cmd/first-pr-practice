# Practice Investment Cases

Work each cold using `07_FRAMEWORKS/Investment_Memo_Template.md`, then check
against the course reasoning noted underneath.

## Case 1 — The staging decision *(founder side)*
A founder has a term sheet for $40M at a $12M pre-money. She could instead raise
$18M now and target $22M at a much higher valuation in 18 months. She is worried
about market risk.
**Work:** build both cap tables. What does each path cost her in final proceeds?
What has to be true for the staged path to be wrong?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-005` — staging produced $16.9M vs $12.4M per founder,
~40% more. The risk is not raising the second round at all.*

## Case 2 — The preference trap
A company has raised $40M across three rounds, all 1x non-participating. It
receives a $38M acquisition offer.
**Work:** who gets what? What if one round had been participating? What if the
last round had a 2x preference?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-005` — preferences cumulate by round; common is worth
approximately nothing.*

## Case 3 — Price the round
A B2B software company will plausibly reach $80M revenue in 5 years. The sector
trades at 4x revenue. You need 40% IRR.
**Work:** maximum post-money today? If the founder wants $120M post, what would
have to change — revenue, multiple, or your required return?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-013` — `(80 × 4) ÷ 5 = $64M`. At $120M you'd need a
much larger exit or accept a lower return.*

## Case 4 — The PE screen
Pick a sector with a clear public leader. Find its EV/EBITDA. Then find the
weakest listed player.
**Work:** what is the gap? Is the weak player broken, or just not scaling? Would
you fix it or roll it up?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-011` — Allbirds at 0.24x revenue vs a 0.55x market
average; Birkenstock at 3.4x revenue and 118x EBITDA.*

## Case 5 — The zero-growth LBO
A profitable business is available at $100M, 10x EBITDA. You can borrow 70% at
9%. It generates $10M EBITDA growing 0%.
**Work:** can it service the debt? What is the sponsor's return at a flat exit in
year 7? At what interest rate does the deal stop working?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-008` — the worked example returned 3.75x with no growth
at all. "Very dependent on the cost of debt."*

## Case 6 — Accretive or dilutive?
Acquirer trades at 20x earnings; target at 14x. All-stock deal at a 25% premium,
no synergies.
**Work:** accretive or dilutive, and why? What synergies would make it neutral?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-013` — buying a lower P/E with stock is generally
accretive; the break-even table gives ~$60M at a 25% premium in the worked case.*

## Case 7 — Diligence red flags
A target shows 40% revenue growth, 25% EBITDA margins, and one customer at 45% of
revenue. Inventory grew 90% while sales grew 20%.
**Work:** what do you investigate first? What would change your price? What would
kill the deal?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-012` — customer concentration and QoE; `SRC-P-009` —
Guitar Center's inventory/sales mismatch preceded bankruptcy.*

## Case 8 — The CVC round
A seed-stage company can take $5M from a financial VC at $20M post, or $5M from a
strategic corporate investor at $28M post. The corporate is also its largest
potential channel partner.
**Work:** what does the founder gain and lose? What would you want in the terms?
*Course reasoning: `SRC-P-006` — CVCs are less valuation sensitive and pay up;
they're also a likely acquirer, which can narrow the buyer field.*

## Case 9 — The Iowa thesis *(build, don't solve)*
Using `SRC-P-010`'s Global Atlantic material and `SRC-P-003`'s Iowa IPO list,
write the investment case for or against private credit's growing role in
insurance-backed retirement products.
**Work:** who benefits, who bears the risk, what would you need to see to get
comfortable? **This is a portfolio piece, not an exercise.**
