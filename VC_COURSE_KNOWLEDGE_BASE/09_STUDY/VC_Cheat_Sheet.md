## THE FORMULAS
```
IRR  = ((FV/PV)^(1/n)) − 1
MOIC = Exit ÷ Invested

PRE + MONEY = POST
MONEY ÷ POST = ownership = founder dilution
POST = INVESTMENT ÷ OWNERSHIP%

VC METHOD:  required post = (exit revenue × exit multiple) ÷ required MOIC

EV = Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash          (+ minority interest)
EV + WC = total needed to BUY AND RUN
EBITDA = Net Income + Interest + Taxes + D&A

Cost of Equity = Rf + (Beta × MRP)           = 4% + 1.25×8% = 14%
WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1−t))       = 9.15% at 2/3 debt

Gross Margin = Revenue − COGS
Rule of 40   = revenue growth% + margin% ≥ 40%   (use FCF/EBITDA)
Goodwill     = Purchase Price − FV of net identifiable assets
EPS          = (Net Income − Dividends) ÷ Shares
```

## THE DISTINCTIONS
**VC vs PE** — VC funds *unprofitable* early companies through the **valley of
death**; PE buys *profitable* companies with debt **the company repays**. VC is a
subset of PE but a totally different process.

**Priced vs unpriced** — preferred equity is priced (needs a valuation);
convertible notes and SAFEs defer it. Note = **debt, senior**. SAFE = **warrant,
not debt, ~15% discount, indefinite**.

**Liquidation preference** — 1x non-participating is standard. Preferences
**cumulate by round**. **Converts automatically at IPO and is forfeited.**

**Anti-dilution** — weighted average (common) vs full ratchet (punitive).

**Cost vs revenue synergies** — cost: 12–24 months, bankable. Revenue: 3–5 years,
a promise. Dis-synergies: always underestimated.

**Comps vs precedents** — comps have no control premium; precedents include
**+20–40%** and read higher.

## THE METAPHORS (his, and the best interview answer you have)
- **Investment bankers = salespeople** — volume, fast, predictable
- **VCs = bettors who sell the house they funded** — slow, unpredictable, massive upside
- **PE = house flippers who profit even without appreciation** — leverage and change

## THE LINES WORTH QUOTING
- *"Similar but mostly different"* — on the asset classes
- *"…or not….."* — the built-in skepticism about private markets
- *"Not understanding the term sheet is the number one source of later issues
  with founders and investors being misaligned, especially on exits"*
- *"Entry multiple is everything — overpay and no operational improvement will
  save you"*
- *"Leverage amplifies whether good or bad"*
- *"Margin covers up a lot of sins"*
- *"Flip the neighborhood, not just the house"*
- *"12 is the new 5"* — PE now needs 12% EBITDA growth where 5% sufficed
- *"You make upside money when you own something valuable"*

## THE CASES
| Case | The lesson |
|---|---|
| **Compaq** | Dilution — founders 5–7% each at IPO after 3 rounds; $500M cap on $111M revenue; DEC at $9.6B then HP at $25B |
| **Sevin Rosen Fund I** | $25M fund held Compaq *and* Lotus → ~$191M. Power law |
| **RJR Nabisco** | $31.4B on $1.5B equity, ~95% debt, ~10-11x entry → only ~14% IRR. Entry multiple |
| **Vista / Marketo** | 22% debt, −31%→+2% margin, 3.1x in 25 months. Operations, not leverage |
| **Guitar Center** | LBO → Chapter 11. Inventory grew 123% CAGR vs 15.8% sales |
| **NVIDIA** | $42M of VC → most valuable US company |
| **Cisco / Cerent** | CVC pathway: partner → invest → acquire |
| **Global Atlantic / KKR** | PE-owned insurers investing in PE-originated private credit. **In Des Moines** |
