# Flashcards

Format: **Q** / **A** / *source*. Drill the L1 block until instant.

## L1 — MUST KNOW

**Q:** VC vs PE in one sentence each?
**A:** VC funds *unprofitable* early-stage companies through the valley of death; PE buys *profitable* companies, often with debt the company itself repays. VC is a subset of PE but a totally different process. *`SRC-P-001`*

**Q:** VC and PE return hurdles?
**A:** VC 40% IRR (4x/4yr, 5x/5yr — "4x4"). PE 20% IRR (2x/4yr — "2x4"). *`SRC-P-013`*

**Q:** Three ways to express pre/post money?
**A:** `PRE + MONEY = POST` · `MONEY ÷ POST = ownership = dilution` · `POST = INVESTMENT ÷ OWNERSHIP%` *`SRC-P-007`, `SRC-P-003`*

**Q:** The Venture Capital Method?
**A:** `required post = (exit revenue × exit multiple) ÷ required MOIC`. Valuation derived backwards from the return you need. *`SRC-P-013`*

**Q:** Why isn't DCF used to value startups?
**A:** "There is often no cash flow." Market averages by round (NVCA, PitchBook) are used instead. *`SRC-P-007`*

**Q:** Standard liquidation preference?
**A:** 1x non-participating. Preferences cumulate by round. Converts automatically at IPO and is forfeited. *`SRC-P-005`*

**Q:** Full ratchet vs weighted average?
**A:** Full ratchet — price down 20%, get 20% more shares; most stringent. Weighted average — that effect times the ratio of new money to old; most common. *`SRC-P-005`*

**Q:** SAFE vs convertible note?
**A:** Note is **debt and senior to preferred**; SAFE is a **warrant, not debt**, compensated by a ~15% discount, with an indefinite term. Neither requires a valuation. *`SRC-P-005`*

**Q:** Enterprise value, and why PE uses it?
**A:** `Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash`. PE buys the whole company including debt; EV is capital-structure neutral. EV in, EV out. *`SRC-P-008`*

**Q:** VC fund economics?
**A:** 2% annual fee on committed capital, 20% carry after the fund is returned, 10-year life, GP commits 1%. *`SRC-P-002`*

**Q:** What share of VC deals return capital?
**A:** ~25%. Three of four do not. *`SRC-P-010`*

**Q:** What share of ventures exit by IPO?
**A:** ~10%. About 90% are acquired — mostly because they could not scale. *`SRC-P-002`*

**Q:** What does it cost to reach an IPO?
**A:** $75M+, at least 3 rounds, ~8 years — inside a 10-year fund. *`SRC-P-002`*

**Q:** The staging result?
**A:** $40M raised in two rounds → $16.9M per founder. All at once at the early valuation → $12.4M. **~40% more from staging.** *`SRC-P-005`*

**Q:** LBO leverage norms?
**A:** 1/3 equity, 2/3 debt. 4–7x Debt/EBITDA, sponsors target 5–6x. Stack ≈ 50/20/25/5. *`SRC-P-008`, `SRC-P-009`*

**Q:** What share of M&A destroys value?
**A:** ~47%; ~73% fail to beat the S&P. Acquirers lose 4–7% over three years; targets gain 20–25%. *`SRC-P-013`*

**Q:** The three players as metaphors?
**A:** Bankers = salespeople (volume, fast, predictable). VCs = bettors who sell the house they funded (slow, unpredictable, massive upside). PE = house flippers who profit even without appreciation. *`SRC-P-001`*

## L2 — SHOULD KNOW

**Q:** Rule of 40?
**A:** Revenue growth% + profit margin% ≥ 40%, using FCF or EBITDA rather than GAAP net income. An IPO-readiness screen. *`SRC-P-007`*

**Q:** Cost vs revenue synergies?
**A:** Cost: 12–24 months, predictable. Revenue: 3–5 years, harder. Dis-synergies: always underestimated. *`SRC-P-012`*

**Q:** Comps vs precedent transactions?
**A:** Comps carry no control premium (minority value). Precedents include +20–40% and read higher. *`SRC-P-013`*

**Q:** Why do CVCs pay more?
**A:** They buy strategic value over immediate gains, so they're less valuation sensitive. Over half of all VC is CVC. *`SRC-P-006`*

**Q:** What do CVCs want instead of a board seat?
**A:** Information rights — the board package without the governance responsibility. *`SRC-P-005`*

**Q:** The CVC pathway?
**A:** GTM partner → investor → acquirer. Cisco/Cerent. *`SRC-P-006`*

**Q:** Platform vs add-on?
**A:** Platform is the first acquisition; add-ons are complementary and usually keep their identity. ~30% of PE deals are platforms, >60% including add-ons. Returns ~20% vs ~15%. *`SRC-P-011`*

**Q:** Why do banker-sourced deals return less?
**A:** Bankers run an auction, which produces more bids and bids the price up. Proprietary sourcing is the edge LPs select on. *`SRC-P-011`*

**Q:** What is private credit and how big?
**A:** Non-bank direct lending. $2T in 2024, funding 77% of LBO debt. Created by post-2008 bank retrenchment. *`SRC-P-010`*

**Q:** How is a hedge fund fee different?
**A:** 2% of NAV marked to market quarterly, not 2% of committed capital — so fee income moves with performance. *`SRC-P-014`*

**Q:** Three cash needs, only one on the P&L?
**A:** Operating losses, working capital, and capex. *`SRC-P-008`*

**Q:** M&A escrow norms?
**A:** 8–15% of purchase price held 1–2 years. Exclusivity is the only binding term in the term sheet. *`SRC-P-012`*

**Q:** IPO mechanics?
**A:** 18–24 months; 5–7% spread; 15% greenshoe; 90–180 day lock-up; banks want 5–10x oversubscription; the pop is deliberate 10–25% underpricing. *`SRC-P-007`*

**Q:** LBO bankruptcy rate?
**A:** ~20% of large LBOs within 10 years, vs 2% for comparable companies — and the research finds PE often buys *healthy* firms and raises their default risk. *`SRC-P-009`, `SRC-P-010`*

**Q:** "12 is the new 5"?
**A:** Without cheap debt, PE must now drive 12% EBITDA growth to earn returns that previously required 5%. *`SRC-P-012`*

## L3 — ADVANCED

**Q:** Where does 2-and-20 come from?
**A:** Alfred Winslow Jones restructured his hedge fund into an LP/GP vehicle with 2% fees and 20% carry in **1952**. VC and PE inherited it. *`SRC-P-014`*

**Q:** Why did hedge fund alpha collapse?
**A:** The industry went from $200B to ~$6T — 30x — and too much capital chasing the same arbitrage compressed spreads to near zero. *`SRC-P-014`*

**Q:** REIT requirements?
**A:** Distribute 90% of taxable income; hold 75%+ of assets in real estate. This avoids double taxation. *`SRC-P-014`*

**Q:** HSR threshold and timing?
**A:** $126.4M; 30-day initial waiting period; a Second Request extends it; 2–5% of deals need remedies. *`SRC-P-012`*

**Q:** When is goodwill written down — and when is it *chosen* to be?
**A:** On worse performance, worse outlook, or higher discount rates. Research suggests impairments are timed strategically, using down markets as cover — "clearing of the decks." *`SRC-P-013`*
