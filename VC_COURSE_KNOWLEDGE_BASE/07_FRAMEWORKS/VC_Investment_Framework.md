# VC Investment Framework

**Version:** v1.0 — built 2026-08-12 from the 13 fully-extracted decks.
**Every element traces to course material.** Gaps are marked, not filled.

---

> **Derived from what the course actually emphasizes**, not from a generic
> template. The weighting below reflects how much airtime each topic received
> across 13 decks — which is itself informative: **this course weights DEAL
> STRUCTURE and RETURNS far more heavily than founder or market assessment.**

## 0. The screen before the screen

**Is this a venture-scale market?** A venture market must grow far faster than
**~2% US GDP** (`SRC-P-006`). *"WHAT PROBLEM ARE YOU SOLVING… that creates a
HIGH GROWTH opportunity?"* — **"NAIL THIS CHART!"**

**Is it in the domain?** VC is *"THE DOMAIN OF UNPROFITABLE, EARLY STAGE
COMPANIES THAT NEED TO BE FINANCED THROUGH THE 'VALLEY OF DEATH'"* (`SRC-P-001`).
Profitable and stable is a PE problem, not a VC one.

## 1. TEAM — *lightly covered; the course's thinnest area*
What the material supports:
- **Leadership Team is a required pitch section** (`SRC-P-006`)
- **Only 41% of founders are still CEO at IPO**, dropping another 20% by year
  three (`SRC-P-007`) — assume the founder is not the endgame CEO
- **Management team evaluation** is named in diligence: *"track records,
  leadership styles, industry expertise. Strong operational management can
  accelerate value creation"* (`SRC-P-012`)
- Coral Ventures' *"the PhD that survived the Killing Fields…….not"* — a
  **background verification failure** (`SRC-P-012`)

`[NOT PROVIDED]` — founder-market fit, coachability, and the qualitative
founder-assessment heuristics most VC firms use. See external layer gap #5.

## 2. MARKET
- **Market Size** and **Market Validation** are both required pitch sections
- Validation evidence the course accepts: real trial data (`SRC-P-006`'s Parkiva
  demo — 90% would use again, 85% 2+ times/year)
- Competitive advantage required; competition research optional
- **"Why Now?"** — *"Its pretty easy to see if something is right……it's very hard
  to time your investment right"*
- Where the money actually goes: **AI dominates VC now; SaaS dominates; VC is
  still Silicon Valley/Bay Area-led and mostly US-based** (`SRC-P-002`)

## 3. PRODUCT & DEFENSIBILITY
**Underlying Magic / Intellectual Property** is a **required** section —
*"Do you have any Patents applied for? Anything most unique about your approach?"*
Technical debt is a real diligence item (`SRC-P-012`): *"shortcuts in software
development [create] hidden maintenance or refactoring costs."*

Litigation risk is survivable: IBM sued Compaq for eight years and *"did not
really work"* (`SRC-P-007`).

## 4. TRACTION
Required: **Traction/Milestones**. `[NOT PROVIDED]` — the course gives no
retention, cohort, or growth-rate thresholds for venture stage. The only
quantitative screen offered is the **Rule of 40** (`SRC-P-007`), which is an
IPO-readiness test, not an early-stage one.

## 5. ECONOMICS
- **Financials 1** — must be the full spreadsheet
- **Financials 2** — **revenue vs net income, explicitly to show the valley of death**
- **Gross margin is the strong business indicator** (`SRC-P-008`): *"margin covers
  up a lot of sins."* High margin tolerates high opex; low margin does not
- **Three cash needs, only one on the P&L**: operating losses, **working
  capital**, **capex**

`[NOT PROVIDED]` — CAC, LTV, payback, burn multiple.

## 6. THE DEAL — *the course's centre of gravity*
This is where the material is deepest, and where an analyst trained on it will
be strongest.

**Price:** derived by the **Venture Capital Method** —
`required post = (exit revenue × exit multiple) ÷ required MOIC`. Cross-checked
against **market averages by round** (NVCA, PitchBook). **DCF is not used.**

**Structure:** priced preferred vs convertible note vs SAFE — decided by *whether
you must price today* and *where you sit in the stack*.

**Terms that decide outcomes:**
| Term | What to check |
|---|---|
| Liquidation preference | 1x non-participating is standard. Participating, 1x-with-2x-cap, and 3x exist. **Preferences cumulate by round.** |
| Convert point | Where preferred is better off as common |
| Anti-dilution | Weighted average (common) vs full ratchet (punitive) |
| Protective provisions | What the preferred can block |
| Pro rata | Right to maintain ownership — *"you can't kick them out of the syndicate"* |
| Information rights | What CVCs want in lieu of a board seat |
| Vesting & acceleration | Full or partial on a liquidity event |

> **The warning that governs this whole section:** *"Not understanding the Term
> Sheet is the NUMBER ONE source of later issues with founders and investors
> being misaligned, especially on EXITS."*

**Dilution:** stage the rounds. `SRC-P-005` proves staging is worth **~40% more**
to founders. A $38M offer against $40M raised leaves common **worth
approximately nothing** — preferences eat it.

## 7. RETURN & EXIT
- **Target:** 40% IRR → **4x in 4 years**, or **5x in 5** (*"what I would model"*)
- **Fund-level:** 20%+ IRR to be top half, or no next fund
- **Path:** **~90% are sold before IPO**, mostly because they could not scale.
  IPO is the best return but the rarer outcome
- **Cost of the IPO path:** $75M+, 3+ rounds, ~8 years — against a 10-year fund
- **Who buys:** *"your Partners are often the ones that are most likely to BUY
  YOUR COMPANY"* — GTM partners are the acquirer pipeline
- **Corporate VC in the round** signals a strategic acquirer and supports a
  higher price

## 8. THE PORTFOLIO REALITY
**~75% of VC deals do not return capital.** NVIDIA took $42M and became the most
valuable US company; another deck traces 8 rounds and ~$150M to a sale at a loss.
**Both outcomes are normal.** Underwrite each deal to a 4–5x, knowing most will
return nothing.

## Decision checklist
- [ ] Venture-scale market growing far above GDP?
- [ ] Unprofitable/early — genuinely a VC problem, not PE?
- [ ] Required post-money by the VC Method ≥ the ask? (If not, pass or negotiate)
- [ ] Comparable to NVCA/PitchBook averages for the round?
- [ ] Gross margin structurally healthy?
- [ ] Cash plan covers losses **+ working capital + capex**?
- [ ] Preference stack survivable in a mediocre exit?
- [ ] Realistic acquirers identifiable — ideally GTM partners?
- [ ] Exit plausible inside the fund's remaining life?
- [ ] Would 4–5x in 4–5 years require heroic or ordinary assumptions?
