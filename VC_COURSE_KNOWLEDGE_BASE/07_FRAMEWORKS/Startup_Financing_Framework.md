## The ladder — as the course teaches it (`SRC-P-002`)

> **"Multiple methods often used in a deal lifecycle. Not mutually exclusive
> options."**

| # | Method | Dilutive? | Key characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bootstrapping** | No | Personal savings, sweat equity, early revenue. **Maximum control retained. Forces lean operations.** Risk: limited growth |
| 2 | **Friends & Family** | Usually | Informal agreements (equity or loan). **"Trust is critical, but risks relationships if venture fails"** |
| 3 | **Crowdfunding** | Varies | **Up to $5M before SEC regulated.** Donation, reward, debt or equity based. **"Great for testing market demand before launch"** |
| 4 | **Incubators & Accelerators** | Small equity | Mentorship, workspace, network. **May include demo day** |
| 5 | **SBIR/STTR Grants** | **NON-DILUTIVE** | US programs for R&D-heavy startups. Competitive, paperwork-heavy. Best for tech/biotech/engineering |
| 6 | **Bank Loans** | No | **"*NOT*"** — collateral and credit history required; interest repaid regardless of success; **"often inaccessible for asset-light startups"** |
| 7 | **Angel Investors** | Yes | Wealthy individuals, own funds. Capital + mentorship + connections. **"THIS MAY BE THE FIRST STEP WITH PREFERRED STOCK"** |
| 8 | **Venture Capital** | Yes | Institutional. See `Fund_Economics_Framework.md` |

**Angels are the hinge.** Everything before is informal or non-dilutive;
everything from angels onward runs on preferred stock and term sheets.

## The stage map (`SRC-P-003`)
```

VC                  →  Institutional investor

```

## Priced vs unpriced — the organizing distinction (`SRC-P-003`)

| | **PRICED** | **UNPRICED (deferred)** |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument | Preferred equity | Convertible note · SAFE |
| Requires a valuation? | **Yes** | **No** |
| Set by | Pre/post money in the term sheet | The *next* round's price |

### The three instruments compared (`SRC-P-005`, `SRC-P-003`)

| | **Preferred (priced)** | **Convertible Note** | **SAFE** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Equity | **Debt** | **A warrant** |
| Valuation needed | **Yes** | No | No |
| Seniority | Below debt, above common | **Senior to preferred — paid back first** | Not debt |
| Compensation for waiting | — | Converts at next round price | **~15% discount** |
| Term | — | Until paid or converted | **Indefinite** |
| Dividends | — | **Accrue but typically unpaid** (`SRC-P-003`) | — |
| Why used | Sets ownership definitively | Defers pricing | **"Very Simple, Very Fast"** |

> **The trade in one line:** must you price the company today, and where do you
> sit in the payout stack?

## The dilution arithmetic
```
PRE + MONEY = POST
MONEY ÷ POST = ownership bought = dilution to founders
POST = INVESTMENT ÷ OWNERSHIP%
```

### ⭐ The staging lesson (`SRC-P-005`) — the most important result in the course
$40M raised two ways:
```
TWO ROUNDS:  $18M at a $12M pre (Series A) + $22M at a $100M pre (Series B)
             → Founders $16.9M each · Investors $381M on $40M (~9x)

ONE ROUND:   all $40M at the $12M Series A valuation
             → Founders $12.4M each · Investors $466M on $40M (~12x)
```
> **Staging is worth ~40% more to each founder.**

`CLAUDE INFERENCE` — the mechanism: capital is not the scarce resource, the
*valuation at which you sell it* is. Raise only what you need to reach the next
valuation step.

## What happens at every round (`SRC-P-003`)
> **"THE COMPANY is RE-VALUED (very arbitrarily) EVERY FINANCING ROUND, and
> OWNERSHIP CAN CHANGE — SIGNIFICANTLY, as well as BOARD/MANAGEMENT OFTEN
> CHANGE."**

Only **41% of founders are still CEO at IPO**, and that drops another 20% by
three years public (`SRC-P-007`).

## What it takes to reach an exit
- **$75M+ and at least 3 rounds** to go public; **~8 years** (`SRC-P-002`)
- **~90% are sold before IPO** — *"most times because they could not scale"*
- *"Statistics show the IPO is the best return for investors and founders. That
  is the goal."*
