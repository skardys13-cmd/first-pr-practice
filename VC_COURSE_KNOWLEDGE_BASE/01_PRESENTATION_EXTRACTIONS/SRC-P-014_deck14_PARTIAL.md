WHAT IS A HEDGE FUND?  

A private pooled fund – from mostly institutions + wealthy investors-

HEDGE FUNDS FACE LITTLE REGULATION FROM THE SEC   

The SEC only requires hedge funds to register if they have more than $150 million in private funds

Only Funds with AUM of $500 million or more must file quarterly and report the details of their liabilities and assets.

The SEC adopted measures to force large hedge funds to disclose more information via Form PF, which is used for confidential event reporting.

So a $100M Hedge funds is unregistered, unregulated, and has no reporting obligations

Timeline:

2000s: Mainstream institutional acceptance

2010s-: Recovery? and continued evolution?

JONES “HEDGED” RISK by LONG/SHORT STRATEGY

Jones was the first money manager to combine:

SHORT SELLING- making money when the equity falls in value

LEVERAGE- to increase financial returns

POOLING INVESTMENTS other investors.

This investing innovation is now called the classic long/short model.

a LP/GP 2% fees

20% carried interest structure

Why it’s called a “Hedge” Fund- making hedging bets

Long and Short Strategies in Hedging

WHAT IS A LONG POSITION?

A long position means you buy a stock expecting its price to go up, so you can sell later at a profit.

It just means buying and holding until you make a profit and sell, and it may be something you hold for a long time in a portfolio

WHAT IS A SHORT SALE?

Short sale of stocks refers to the sale of a stock borrowed for a fee and time slot, then immediately sold, then repurchased ….hopefully at a lower price…then returned.

Short selling involves borrowing shares from a broker or another investor to sell in the market.

Short selling allows investors TO PROFIT FROM FALLING STOCK PRICES.
