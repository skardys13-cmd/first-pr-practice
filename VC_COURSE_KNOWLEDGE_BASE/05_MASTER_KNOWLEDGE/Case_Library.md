## 1. COMPAQ / *Silicon Cowboys* — the flagship VC case ⭐
**Sources:** `SRC-P-001` (setup), `SRC-P-007` (full scorecard)
**Format:** documentary watched outside class + written assignment + in-class
"checking the score."

**Situation.** Founded 1982 by three friends in a Houston diner to build a
portable PC against IBM.

**Financing** — `SOURCE FACT`
```
$0.003   Seed       Founders
$2.5M    Series A   Sevin Rosen
$5.0M    Series B   Sevin Rosen, IVP, others
$25M     Series C   Sevin Rosen, IVP, others
```
**"*(NO BANK LOANS AT ALL — MOVIE IS WRONG)*"**

**The scorecard.** Founders held **5–7% each** at IPO. Market cap **~$500M**
(~$1.7B today) on **$111M revenue** = **~5x revenue**. Founder sellable value
**$25–35M each**. *"More than they would have made opening a Mexican
restaurant………"*

**Fund economics.** Sevin Rosen Fund I was **$25M** and held **both Compaq and
Lotus** — "the first laptop, first spreadsheet." Gross proceeds **~$191M**.

**Aftermath.** Fastest to Fortune 500, $1B sales by 1987; passed IBM by 1995;
**bought DEC for $9.6B in 1998 ("bad fit")**; **HP acquired Compaq for ~$25B in
2002.** IBM litigated 1983–1991 and *"did not really work."*

**Lessons.** Dilution is brutal even in triumph · a great IPO isn't a great
company forever · fund returns come from a handful of holdings · bad M&A kills
market leaders · popular media gets venture finance wrong.

**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ Best answer for *"tell me about a company you analyzed,"*
*"walk me through a deal,"* and *"explain dilution."*

## 2. RJR NABISCO / *Barbarians at the Gate* — the flagship PE case ⭐
**Sources:** `SRC-P-008`, `SRC-P-009`, `SRC-P-010`

**Why it was a target.** Strong tobacco cash flow. *"The Market didn't reward
them for better and better financial results…..**IT'S A TOBACCO COMPANY**……became
a liability."* Management thought they could do better owning it.

**The deal.** **$31.4B total** — KKR equity **$1.5B (5%)**, bank loans **$14.9B
(47%)**, junk bonds **$15.0B (48%)**. Share price **$55 → $109** = **98%
premium**. Entry **~10–11x EBITDA** on an asset that historically traded at **5x**.
**KKR itself contributed only ~0.3%**; its LPs co-invested directly.

**The math.**
```
Entry 1989:  EV $31.4B · Net debt $29.9B · Equity $1.5B · EBITDA ~$3.0B
Exit ~1995:  EV ~$26B  · Net debt ~$18B  · Equity ~$8B
$1.5B → ~$8B over ~6 years = ~14% IRR   (vs a 20–25% target)
```

**The fees.** KKR took a **1% transaction fee ($200M)** *plus* fund-level
2-and-20 — **~$1B total, irrespective of returns.** *"That's the main reason they
ended up with a 14% IRR despite the company getting sold in pieces."*

**The outcome.** Cash flow couldn't service 8x leverage. Divestitures began within
a year; **only ~$9B of ~$23B+ debt was ever repaid.**

**Why they didn't take the highest bid** — `PROFESSOR / COURSE VIEW`:
*"In my opinion? Because **Henry Kravis wanted the notoriety**…as the 'inventor'
of LBO… **his hubris cost KKR**."*

**Lessons.** **Entry multiple is everything** · fees can make a bad deal fine for
the sponsor · auctions bid prices past rationality · conflicted management ·
leverage amplifies both ways.

**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ The definitive "what makes a bad deal" answer.

## 3. VISTA EQUITY × MARKETO — the model good PE deal ⭐
**Source:** `SRC-P-010`

Acquired **May 2016 for $1.79B** (62% premium); sold to **Adobe Sept 2018 for
$4.75B**. **~3.1x MOIC, ~72% IRR, 25-month hold.**

**Why it was a target.** Growth had decelerated **64% → 15%** — *"classic value
unlock opportunity."*

**How it was financed.** **Only 22% debt** ($400M vs $1,390M equity) — against
Toys "R" Us at ~85% and RJR at ~90%.

**What Vista did.** New CEO (Steve Lucas) in month five · cut bloated S&M
(*"60¢ of every dollar"*) · cloud migration · **net margin −31% → +2%** ·
revenue $241M → $321M.

**The three levers:** revenue/EBITDA growth **~45%** · multiple expansion
**~35%** (bought at 7.4x revenue, sold at **14.8x** — Adobe needed to beat
Salesforce) · margin improvement **~20%**.

**Lessons.** Low leverage was the foundation · operations beat engineering ·
**strategic exit timing is a value lever** · 3.1x in 25 months vs a typical 5–7
year hold.

**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ Pair with RJR — same asset class, opposite method.
Especially strong given *"12 is the new 5."*

## 4. GUITAR CENTER — LBO to bankruptcy
**Source:** `SRC-P-009`

1997 IPO at $15, 39 stores → **2007 Bain acquires for $1.9B** (26% premium), 214
stores, **+$1.6B debt, +$152M annual interest** → 2014 Ares swaps debt for
control → **2020 Chapter 11**, eliminating ~$800M of ~$1.3B debt.

**The warning sign visible beforehand:** **inventory grew 123% CAGR vs 15.8%
sales CAGR (2004–2006)**; ~$2.0M of inventory per new store × 33 openings = ~$66M.

**Lesson.** Leverage without operational improvement. And working capital
intensity — the cash need that never appears on the P&L — was visible in the
financials before the deal.

**Interview use:** ⭐⭐ The middle case between RJR and Marketo.

## 6. CISCO / CERENT — the CVC pathway
**Source:** `SRC-P-006`

*"Those firms may also be your **Go To Market partner first**….. Then they make an
**investment**…..like Cisco led a VC round in Cerent….. Then they **buy the
company**…..like Cisco bought Cerent."*

**Lesson.** Partner → invest → acquire. A CVC investor is simultaneously
distribution and a likely exit — and explains why CVCs tolerate higher valuations.
**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ for any CVC conversation.

## 7. CALIX / CLEARFIELD — a first-hand divestiture
**Source:** `SRC-P-011`

```
2010 IPO raised $83M
2018 Q1:  $39.8M "cash" — but a $30M credit line was tapped out and had
          BROKEN ITS COVENANT
          → $9.8M actual cash − $5.6M expected burn = $3.2M real buffer
```
*"WE NEEDED EMERGENCY CASH — so what did we do? **We DIVESTED the LOWEST GROSS
MARGIN PRODUCT LINE WE HAD** to Clearfield."*

**Lessons.** Headline cash ≠ available cash · a covenant breach turns a credit
line into a liability · divestiture as a financing tool, selling the lowest-margin
unit first.
**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ Excellent for *"how do you read a balance sheet
skeptically?"*

## 8. NVIDIA vs THE 24-YEAR LOSS — the power law in two slides
**Source:** `SRC-P-002`

**NVIDIA:** *"VC's THAT PUT IN $42M to TAKE IT PUBLIC IN 1999"* — now the most
valuable US company.
**The other:** 8 rounds (A–H), **~$150M invested**, IPO at $13, dot-com crash,
**sold to Calix at a loss**, 24 years. `[INTERPRETATION UNCERTAIN]` — very likely
Occam Networks.

**Lesson.** Same industry, same era, opposite outcomes. **~75% of VC deals don't
return capital.** Underwrite to 4–5x knowing most return nothing.
**Interview use:** ⭐⭐⭐ The best available answer on venture return distribution.

## 10. ALLBIRDS vs BIRKENSTOCK — the PE screen
**Sources:** `SRC-P-011`, `SRC-P-012`

BIRD: $37.37M cap on $160M revenue = **0.24x** (market average **0.55x**).
BIRK: $7.4B = **3.4x revenue = 118x EBITDA**.

**Lesson.** The relative-multiple screen, and why revenue multiples get used when
earnings are thin.
