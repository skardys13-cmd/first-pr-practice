## Silicon Cowboys / Compaq — the flagship VC case

**Format** — a documentary film watched outside class, followed by a written
assignment and an in-class "checking the score" wrap-up (`SRC-P-007`). The class
plan in `SRC-P-005` shows it occupying a no-class week with a write-up due.

### Situation
Compaq, founded 1982, builds an IBM-compatible portable PC and goes public in
1983 — one of the largest IPOs of its time.

### The financing history — `SOURCE FACT`
```
Seed        $0.003          Founders
Series A    $2.5M           Sevin Rosen
Series B    $5.0M           Sevin Rosen, IVP, Others
Series C    $25M            Sevin Rosen, IVP, Others
```
**"3 Institutional VC Rounds after SEED"** — and the deck flags in capitals:
**"*(NO BANK LOANS AT ALL - MOVIE IS WRONG)*"**

### The scorecard — `SOURCE FACT`
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Founder ownership at IPO | Estimated **5–7% each** for the three founders |
| IPO market cap | **~$500M** (~$1.7B in today's dollars) |
| Revenue at IPO | **$111M** → **~5x revenue** |
| Founder sellable share value at IPO | **~$25–35M each** ($85–115M today) |

The deck notes actual S-1 filings are not digitally stored, so ownership is
estimated — the course is explicit about the limits of its own numbers.

### The GP economics — `SOURCE FACT`
Sevin Rosen Fund I was **$25M** (the Series A fund) and held **both Compaq and
Lotus** — "in its first fund, it invested in the first laptop, first spreadsheet
software." Gross proceeds from Fund I companies: **~$191M estimated**. GP takes
**2% annual fees and 20% carry** above payback of original capital and fees.

### The aftermath — `SOURCE FACT`
- Fastest company to Fortune 500 and **$1B annual sales by 1987**
- By **1995**, surpassed IBM as the world's largest PC supplier
- 1995–1998: slid, strained financials, price wars (Dell named)
- **1998: acquired DEC for ~$9.6B** — "Bad fit…"
- **2002: HP acquired Compaq for ~$25 billion**
- Founders out; "A Home Run at IPO…..but a wild public ride….."

### The IBM litigation thread — `SOURCE FACT`
| Event | Date | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Compaq founded | 1982 | IBM clones in development |
| IBM files first lawsuits | 1983–1984 | Alleged copyright/trademark infringement |
| Series of minor suits | 1984–1986 | Mostly around BIOS compatibility |
| Court rulings favor Compaq | 1986–1987 | Compaq wins on reverse engineering legality |

"Legal efforts by IBM….Did not really work."

### Lessons the case is built to deliver
1. **Dilution is brutal even in a triumph** — three rounds took founders to
   5–7% each. This is the course's most concrete dilution lesson.
2. **A great IPO is not a great company forever** — the 20-year public run
   included the acquisition that broke it.
3. **Fund returns are made by a handful of holdings** — one $25M fund, two
   historic companies, ~7.6x gross.
4. **Popular media gets venture finance wrong** — the bank-loan correction is
   flagged in capitals, consistent with the instructor's Shark Tank remark.
5. **Bad M&A kills market leaders** — DEC at $9.6B preceded the HP sale.

### Interview use
Strong. It supports "tell me about a company you analyzed," "walk me through a
deal," "explain dilution," and "how does a VC make money" with real numbers.

**Source** — `SRC-P-007`, partial extract; class plan in `SRC-P-005`.
