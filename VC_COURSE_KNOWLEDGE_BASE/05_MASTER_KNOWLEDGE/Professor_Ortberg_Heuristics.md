## "Similar but mostly different"

**Category:** investment | **Confidence:** explicit on slide

`SOURCE FACT` (`SRC-P-001`) — among the course objectives, on the relationship
between private equity, venture capital, hedge funds, REITs and infrastructure:
*"The similarities and the differences…similar but mostly different"*

**Why it matters** — the course's organizing premise is that these asset classes
get lumped together as "private markets" and should not be. Directly answers the
common interview question "what's the difference between VC and PE?"

## "…or not….."

**Category:** investment | **Confidence:** explicit on slide

`SOURCE FACT` (`SRC-P-001`) — the course summary describes private market
investing as *"exploring the critical roles in driving innovation and economic
growth…or not….."*

**Why it matters** — this trailing qualifier is the course's stance in
miniature: it will present the industry's self-justification and then test it.
Borne out by `SRC-P-011`, an entire segment on companies that went bankrupt
within three years of a PE buyout.

**Category:** mistakes to avoid | **Confidence:** strongly implied, twice

## The 40% rule of thumb

**Category:** valuation | **Confidence:** explicit on slide

`SOURCE FACT` (`SRC-P-013`) — "RECALL RULE OF THUMB": 2x in 2 years, 3x in 3
years, and 4x in 4 years all approximate **~40% IRR**.

**Why it matters** — the single most portable piece of mental math in the
course. See `Formula_Library.md`.

## Failure belongs on the track record

**Category:** investment | **Confidence:** `CLAUDE INFERENCE`

`SOURCE FACT` (`SRC-P-001`) — the instructor's portfolio list includes
**"BroadRiver- VOICE over IP Service. Chapter 7."** among sixteen entries, most
of them exits.

`CLAUDE INFERENCE` — presenting a total loss openly on a self-introduction slide
models that portfolio losses are a normal, expected outcome rather than
something to hide. Not stated as a rule; inferred from the choice to include it.
