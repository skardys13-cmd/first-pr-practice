## ON THE INDUSTRY ITSELF

### "Similar but mostly different" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
On the relationship between PE, VC, hedge funds, REITs and infrastructure.
The course's organizing premise: they get lumped together and should not be.

### "…or not….." — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
The course summary describes private markets as *"driving innovation and economic
growth…**or not…..**"* The built-in skepticism, delivered in the first five
slides and paid off in the PE bankruptcy material.

### "Don't use those phrases incorrectly" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
> *"Venture Capital is [a] subset of Private Equity — but **NOT the same thing.**
> VC firms and PE firms are **TOTALLY different processes.**"*

### "All that success….from a largely unsuccessful process" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
On VC producing 77% of US public market cap while most individual deals fail.

### The three players, as metaphors — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
Titled **"My Observed Comparisons/Metaphors"**:
- **Investment Bankers are like SALES PEOPLE** — paid on transaction size and
  volume, quickly, **predictably**
- **Venture Capitalists are like BETTORS THAT SELL THE HOUSE THEY FUNDED** — high
  risk, high reward, long term, **slowly but with massive upside, unpredictable**
- **Private Equity Investors are HOUSE FLIPPERS THAT SELL PROFITABLY, EVEN
  WITHOUT APPRECIATION** — opportunists using leverage

**The best interview answer in the course** for "what's the difference between
VC, PE and banking?"

## ON RETURNS AND FUNDS

### The 40% / 20% rules of thumb — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`, `SRC-P-009`, `SRC-P-013`)
See `Formula_Library.md`. **"2x4"** for PE, **"4x4"** for VC. *"5x in 5 years…
this is what I would model when looking at a deal."*

### "No sufficient returns? No more funds." — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-001`)
> *"Many venture firms are **one fund and done, permanently.**"*
Explains why VCs behave as they do: fund-level survival pressure, not deal-level
greed.

### "Bigger is NOT better…..if they do not have good returns" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-002`)
On fund size. Median VC fund ~$150M.

### "Entry multiple is everything" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-009`)
> *"Overpay and no operational improvement will save you."* KKR's RJR equity grew
> 5x and still returned only ~14% IRR.

### "12 is the New 5" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-012`, citing Bain)
> *"PE returns are harder to achieve without cheap debt; firms must drive **12%
> EBITDA growth** to achieve returns that previously required only **5%**."*

Explains in one line why the operational model displaced the leverage model.

### Failure belongs on the track record — `INFERRED` (`SRC-P-001`)
His own portfolio slide lists **"BroadRiver — VOICE over IP Service. Chapter 7."**
openly among sixteen entries. Not stated as a rule; modelled by the choice to
include it.

## ON DEALS AND STRUCTURE

### ⭐ "Not understanding the Term Sheet is the NUMBER ONE source of later issues" — `EXPLICIT`
(`SRC-P-002`, `SRC-P-003` — flagged as **"My observation"**)
> *"…with founders and investors being misaligned, **especially on EXITS.**"*

**The strongest statement of why the term sheet material matters.**

### "The company is re-valued (very arbitrarily) every financing round" — `EXPLICIT`
(`SRC-P-003`; *"somewhat arbitrarily"* in `SRC-P-007`) — and with it, ownership
can change significantly, as can board and management.

### "Leverage amplifies…whether good or bad" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-008`)
> *"Can they afford it? ........sometimes yes….sometimes it kills the company."*

### Banks do not fund startups — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-002`)
The slide is titled **"6. Banks Loans. *NOT*"** — interest must be repaid
regardless of success, and banks are *"often inaccessible for asset-light
startups."*

### "Using legal strategies is often ineffective" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-007`)
> *"…as it takes so long and the outcome is unknown."* IBM sued Compaq for eight
> years and *"did not really work."*

### Corporate VCs are less valuation sensitive — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-006`)
> *"If you can get a Corporate Venture Capital Arm to lead your financing
> round……you will likely get a higher valuation."* Because they buy strategic
> value, not just return.

### Your partners are your acquirers — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-006`)
> *"The partner you seek…….are likely your best way to GO TO MARKET… **AND…..your
> Partners are often the ones that are most likely to BUY YOUR COMPANY.**"*
The Cisco → Cerent pattern: partner, then invest, then acquire.

## ON SOURCING AND SELECTION

### "Flip the neighborhood ….not just the House" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-011`)
The platform/add-on rollup thesis in one line.

### Proprietary sourcing beats bankers — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-011`)
> *"The NON Investment Banker sourced deals yield better returns for PE — because
> Bankers create an 'auction'… [that] lessens [the] ability to find a deal that
> has a price that has not been 'bid up'."*
With the LP quote: *"We only invest in managers who don't rely solely on bankers."*

### The deal selection method — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-011`)
> Pick a space → find a best-in-class **reference point** for what good trades at
> → *"GO TO YAHOO FINANCE AND START COMPARING THEIR INCOME STATEMENT to the TOP
> PLAYERS. **IS THERE EBITDA? ANYTHING STAND OUT AS BROKEN OR NOT SCALING?
> FIX EM. Or ROLL THEM UP.**"*

### "Easy to see if something is right……very hard to time your investment right" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-006`)

### Companies invite their own buyout — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-010`)
High-cash-flow companies could pre-empt an LBO by returning capital. They don't,
because **"Holding cash = more control = more empire-building."**

## ON M&A

### "M&A is only rational if 1 + 1 > 2" — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-012`)

### Cost synergies are bankable; revenue synergies are a promise — `IMPLIED` (`SRC-P-012`)
Cost: 12–24 months, *"more predictable."* Revenue: *"harder to achieve — take 3–5
years."* Dis-synergies: *"often underestimated in models."*

### Integration is where it falls apart — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-012`)
> *"Effective leadership transition and management of post merger integration….
> **this is where it usually falls apart**……"*

### M&A's hit rate looks like VC's, not PE's — `EXPLICIT` (`SRC-P-012`)
> VC: 3 of 4 deals do NOT return capital. PE: 3 of 4 DO.
> **M&A: 3 of 4 do NOT create shareholder value.**
Posed to the class as a question first — the course's best framing device.
