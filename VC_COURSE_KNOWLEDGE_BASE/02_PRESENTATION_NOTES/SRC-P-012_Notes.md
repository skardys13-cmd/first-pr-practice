## DUE DILIGENCE

### When it happens — `SOURCE FACT`
> "Typically, **Due Diligence follows the signing of an exclusive Letter of
> Intent**. Similar to a Venture Capital Term Sheet….but **LOI for M&A is
> typically more in depth than a VC term sheet** — adding terms for personnel
> issues/retention, compensation.
>
> **A Virtual Data Room is the tool used for this stage.**"

### The three core workstreams — `SOURCE FACT`
"The most critical phase — **uncovers risks that change price or kill deals**"

| **Financial DD** | **Legal DD** | **Operational DD** |
|---|---|---|
| Audit of financials 3–5 years | Contracts & commitments | Operational efficiency metrics |
| **Quality of Earnings (QoE)** | Pending/threatened litigation | **Customer concentration risk** |
| Working capital normalization | IP ownership & patents | Supplier dependencies |
| **Off-balance-sheet liabilities** | Regulatory compliance history | IT systems & cybersecurity |
| Revenue recognition policies | **Change-of-control provisions** | **Key employee retention risk** |

Staffing note: "**3rd Party often hired**" for legal and operational;
"**3rd Party sometimes for Quality of Earnings**"; the middle is internal focus.

### The data room checklist — `SOURCE FACT`

**Corporate and Legal** — articles of incorporation and bylaws · organizational
charts, shareholder agreements · pending or past litigation
**Financial Records** — audited financials for the last three years · AR, AP and
inventory reports · detailed CapEx history and forecasts
**Market and Commercial** — market research and growth projections · customer
contracts, **churn rates**, feedback · competitive analysis
**Operational Metrics** — production workflows and supply chain · quality control
procedures and certifications · inventory turnover
**Technology and IP** — software architecture diagrams and IT security policies ·
patents, trademarks, licensing agreements · technology roadmap
**Human Resources** — management bios and org structure · compensation, benefits,
**turnover rates** · confidentiality and non-compete agreements
**Risk Management** — insurance coverage (general liability, cybersecurity) ·
crisis management and business continuity · **ESG considerations**
**Growth Strategy** — upcoming product launches or market expansion · **M&A
targets under consideration** · cost-reduction strategies

### Commercial DD — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Market Sizing and Growth Trends** — "confirm actual market size and estimate
  growth rates"
- **Competitive Landscape** — "**Independent interviews with customers** can
  highlight the target's market reputation"
- **Customer Mix Analysis** — "revenue concentration, loyalty patterns, product
  adoption. **Heavy reliance on a handful of customers can create vulnerability
  if a large account leaves.**"
- **Pricing Strategies and Value Proposition** — "Spot potential mismatches that
  hinder expansion or open avenues for premium pricing"

### Financial DD — four items, each with a war story — `SOURCE FACT`

1. **Quality of Earnings (QoE)** — "Analyze revenue streams to **distinguish
   recurring revenue from one-time or seasonal sources.** Evaluate gross margins,
   operating expenses, and COGS to verify the sustainability of profit margins."
2. **Working Capital Requirements** — "Determine whether the target maintains
   sufficient liquidity to support operations. **Excessive reliance on
   short-term debt can escalate financial risk.**"
   > **`(EV + WC = TOTAL NEEDED TO BUY AND RUN)`**
3. **Debt Structure** — "Study short- and long-term obligations, noting interest
   rates, covenants, and maturity schedules."
   > **`(COVENANTS YANK LOANS IF CASH TOO LOW/SLOW…DANGEROUS…Picolight example)`**
4. **Forecast Accuracy** — "Assess whether management's financial projections
   stand on realistic assumptions. **Overly optimistic forecasts can inflate
   valuations.**"
   > **`(OCCAM)`**

**The parenthetical annotations are the instructor's own portfolio companies**
(`SRC-P-001`) being used as cautionary examples. **EV + WC = total needed to buy
and run** is a genuinely useful formulation not stated anywhere else in the set.

### IT / Systems / Technology DD — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Infrastructure and Security** — "Outdated or unsecure systems can lead to
  costly breaches"
- **Software Architecture and Scalability** — "Confirm whether the target's
  platform can handle increased user demand"
- **Technical Debt / Field Liabilities** — "**Check whether shortcuts in software
  development created hidden maintenance or refactoring costs.** Excessive
  technical debt diminishes the value of the company's tech assets."
- **IT Governance, Compliance, Regulations** — data protection, disaster
  recovery, system upgrades

### Investment management DD — `SOURCE FACT`
**Management Team Evaluation** ("Strong operational management can accelerate
value creation") · **Operational Efficiency** · **Regulatory and Legal Compliance
with SEC** · **Cultural Fit** ("Culture clashes often lead to high employee
turnover and lost productivity")

## THE SYNERGY PROMISE

`SOURCE FACT` — **"M&A is only rational if 1 + 1 > 2"**

| Type | Description | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Synergies** | Cross-selling, new markets, pricing power | **"Harder to achieve — take 3–5 years to realize"** |
| **Cost Synergies** | Headcount reduction, facility consolidation, procurement savings | **"More predictable; realized within 12–24 months"** |
| **Financial Synergies** | Lower cost of capital, tax benefits (**NOLs**), improved debt capacity | — |
| **Dis-synergies** | Costs of integration, customer defection, employee attrition | **"Often underestimated in models"** |

**The revenue-vs-cost distinction is the practical heart of it**: cost synergies
are bankable, revenue synergies are a promise. Ties to `SRC-P-013`'s break-even
synergy table — if a deal needs revenue synergies to break even, be skeptical.

## THE 2026 PRIVATE EQUITY LANDSCAPE

`SOURCE FACT` — source cited: **Bain and Co.**

- **A "Thawed" Market** — "The **'Great Hesitation'** has ended as rate cuts in
  late 2025/2026 improved deal viability, following three years of subdued
  activity."
- **Record Dry Powder** — "Global PE dry powder hit a record **$3.7 trillion** in
  early 2026, roughly **doubling over the past five years**."
- **Concentrated Capital** — "**Over 40% of dry powder is considered 'aged'**
  (available for 2+ years), with **top 20 managers holding over $600 billion** in
  undeployed capital."
- **Deal Activity Rebound** — "**Buyout deal value jumped 44% to $904B in 2025**,
  driven by large public-to-private takeovers, yet **deal volume (number of
  deals) remains lower**."
- **Exits & Liquidity** — "**Exit values surged 47% in 2025**, yet **distributions
  to LPs remain low**, forcing continued reliance on **secondaries and
  continuation funds**."

### Structural shifts for 2026 — `SOURCE FACT`
- **"12 is the New 5"** — **"PE returns are harder to achieve without cheap debt;
  firms must drive 12% EBITDA growth to achieve returns that previously required
  only 5% growth."**
- **Fundraising Challenges** — "slowed **30%+ from 2023 peaks**; capital is
  concentrating in larger, proven funds while **first-time funds struggle**"
- **K-Shaped Recovery** — "a sharp divide between top-quartile managers and the
  rest, with **'middle-market' firms facing heavy competition**"
- **AI and Operational Focus** — "shifting from **multiple expansion** to **value
  creation**, using AI to re-engineer portfolio company operations"
- **Strategic Focus** — "Sector bets are narrowing toward **tech, industrial
  automation, and insurance**, with higher scrutiny on entry multiples"

### The three takeaways — `SOURCE FACT`
1. **"The Problem is Deployment"** — "Too much money is chasing high-quality
   assets, forcing creative deal-making (**carve-outs, take-privates**)."
2. **"The Shift in Value Creation"** — "Returns are now driven by **operational
   excellence rather than cheap financing.**"
3. **"The LP Pressure"** — "LPs need liquidity back, leading to a **crowded
   secondary market**."

**"12 is the New 5" is the single most current, most quotable line in the entire
deck set** — it explains in one sentence why the Vista/Marketo operational model
(`SRC-P-010`) has displaced the RJR leverage model.

## SECONDARY MARKETS

`SOURCE FACT` — "A marketplace where investors buy and sell **pre-existing
commitments to private funds or direct shares in private companies**. Unlike the
'primary market,' where capital flows directly from investors to a company or a
new fund, **secondaries provide a 'release valve' for liquidity**, allowing
earlier investors (Limited Partners) or **employees** to exit before a company
goes public or is acquired."

"The secondary market has evolved from a **niche tool for distressed sellers**
into a **mainstream portfolio management strategy, growing roughly fourfold over
the last decade** (2014–2024)."

**Implications** — `SOURCE FACT`:
- "**Employees can opt to sell shares before going public**"
- "LPs can sell stakes in funds and/or companies before going public"
- "Companies can sell shares as part of a financing — e.g. **raise $50M round…
  use $45M for company and $5M for buying existing shares**"

**Directly relevant to the dilution material** in `SRC-P-005` — secondaries are
how liquidity happens without an exit.

## M&A STRATEGY

### It starts with strategy — `SOURCE FACT`
> "**STRATEGY leads to three basic Implementation choices: BUILD, BUY, or
> BORROW**"

| Choice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BUILD** | "'Organic' Growth — Internally Created." New product development; hire/create R&D team |
| **BUY** | Acquire a company with the product and/or R&D team |
| **BORROW/SHARE** | "'Re-sell' someone else's product as yours" |

BUY and BORROW are **"InOrganic Growth."**

### Organic vs M&A — `SOURCE FACT`
| **ORGANIC GROWTH** | **M&A (INORGANIC)** |
|---|---|
| Build internally at own pace | Immediate scale & market share |
| Full control over direction | Acquire talent/IP instantly |
| Culture preservation | **Eliminate a competitor** |
| **No integration risk** | Enter new markets fast |
| **No premium paid to sellers** | Access new technology/brands |
| **TIME COST: Years** | **TIME COST: Months** |

### Six strategic imperatives for M&A — `SOURCE FACT`
1. **Speed to Market** — "Building a business unit from scratch can take **5–10
   years**. An acquisition collapses that to months."
2. **Buy vs. Build Economics** — "If the cost to replicate an asset organically
   exceeds the acquisition price, buying is rational."
3. **Eliminating Competition** — "neutralize a rival, reducing competitive
   pressure and increasing pricing power"
4. **Capability Gaps** — "Patents, proprietary tech, or specialized talent
   cannot always be created internally"
5. **Geographic Expansion** — "instant distribution, regulatory licenses, and
   local brand recognition"
6. **Vertical Integration** — "Controlling suppliers or distributors reduces
   costs, secures supply chains, improves margins"

## Deal Types

### Merger vs Acquisition — `SOURCE FACT`
**Similarities:** combine businesses to create value · require due diligence &
valuation · may involve regulatory approval · integration of people, systems,
operations

| **MERGER** | **ACQUISITION** |
|---|---|
| Two companies combine **as equals** | One company **buys** another |
| New or blended leadership | Acquirer retains decision authority |
| Often new company name/brand | Brand often remains acquirer's |
| **Shared control and governance** | Target may be absorbed or kept separate |
| Emphasis on **synergy & collaboration** | Emphasis on **growth & control** |

### Merger types — `SOURCE FACT`
| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Horizontal** | Competitors in the same industry; reduces competition, increases market share | **T-Mobile and Sprint** |
| **Vertical** | Two companies at different stages of the same supply chain | **PC company buys its processor chip supplier** |
| **Conglomerate** | Different industries; diversification, no competition or supply relation | A tech company merging with a food brand |
| **Market-Extension** | Different geographic markets | A European bank merging with a U.S. bank |
| **Product-Extension** | Related products; expands offerings | A snack company merging with a beverage company |

### Asset acquisition — `SOURCE FACT`
"Selling **specific assets** instead of the entire company. **Reduces operational
burden of having to take over the entire company and its employees, liabilities,
etc.** Raises capital quickly."
> **"VERY COMMON IN DEALS WITH A LARGE SIZE DIFFERENCE, and in VENTURE CAPITAL
> EXITS"**

*(This is the structure used in the Dell/Alvarri earnout — `SRC-P-013`.)*

### Friendly vs hostile — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Friendly** — "Both companies agree on terms… negotiated buyouts."
  Example: **Disney acquiring Pixar**
- **Hostile** — "Acquiring company **bypasses management/objections**, often
  through stock purchases." Example: **Kraft's hostile takeover of Cadbury**

### Reverse merger / SPAC — `SOURCE FACT`
"**Private company acquires a public company — buy a 'SHELL'.** Avoids lengthy
IPO process. Gains stock market listing quickly."

## THE M&A TERM SHEET

`SOURCE FACT` — five sections, marked against the VC term sheet:

| # | Section | vs VC |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Value or Purchase Price** — "where the purchase price is defined along with what is being purchased. The form of payment is also included, such as cash or stock. Prospective buyers also commonly add a **working capital peg**……" | **SAME AS VC** |
| 2 | **Employee Matters** — "how the target company's employees will be transitioned post-close… non-compete agreements and potential severances. **Buyers may like to set aside additional money outside of the purchase price to retain key employees.**" | **NEW TO M&A** |
| 3 | **Holdback or Escrow** — "hold back a portion of the purchase price from the seller as **security against any misrepresentations, undisclosed liabilities, or breaches of warranties.** Generally **8–15% of the purchase price**, held for **1–2 years**." | **NEW TO M&A** |
| 4 | **Exclusivity** — "**one of the only portions that is binding once signed.** Restricts the seller from negotiating offers from other prospective buyers for a specific period." | **SAME AS VC** |
| 5 | **Confidentiality** | **SAME AS VC** |

## THE M&A PROCESS — and why it's tricky in public

`SOURCE FACT`:
```
Term Sheet                Due Diligence   DEFINITIVE AGREEMENT   Deal        CLOSED
NOT BINDING          →                 →  IS BINDING          →  Approvals →
(except Exclusivity)                      - Must be publicly
                                            disclosed
                                          …but its not closed yet!
```
> "**A publicly known plan, that may or may not happen. CAN AFFECT THE
> BUSINESS!**"

**An underrated practical point** — the definitive agreement must be disclosed
before closing, so customers, employees and competitors all learn about a deal
that might still fail.

## Antitrust — the DOJ's role

`SOURCE FACT` — **"2–5% of deals not approved without 'remedies'"**

**Pre-filing:** assess whether the transaction meets the **Hart-Scott-Rodino
(HSR) Act** threshold for mandatory filing. **"HSR Act's minimum
size-of-transaction threshold is $126.4 million."** If above, conduct an
antitrust risk assessment; **both parties must file HSR Forms with DOJ and the
FTC**, providing financials, market impact, and business rationale.

**"The Deal Stops…..and waits."** — **Formal DOJ Initial Waiting Period (30
Days)**: DOJ and FTC determine which agency reviews based on industry expertise;
preliminary antitrust review; DOJ can allow the deal to proceed or **request more
information via a Second Request** (extends review).

**DOJ decision:** clear the merger · **negotiate a settlement (e.g. requiring the
sale of assets)** · **challenge the merger in court**

**If litigated,** companies can abandon the deal, defend it in court, or propose
modifications.

### Breakup fees — `SOURCE FACT`
> "Breakup Fees protect those that could feel negative impact of the deal falling
> apart. **Many customers may delay or change business with the company based on
> the acquirer.**
>
> Standard breakup fees: paid when a party **voluntarily** terminates — a buyer
> backing out or a seller accepting a higher bid.
>
> **If the DOJ blocks the deal…… and no specific clause covers this scenario, no
> breakup fee may be owed. BUT…..if not specified otherwise, the buyer could have
> to pay the seller if the deal fails due to regulatory issues.**
>
> **Example: Qualcomm had to pay a $2B breakup fee to NXP when their deal was
> blocked by regulators.**"

## Lessons from failed mergers — `SOURCE FACT`

- **Thorough due diligence** — "Due diligence lapses are a common factor"
- **Comprehensive system and supply integration** — "**can lose customers quickly
  if this is not done right on Day 1**…..and if the two companies are on
  different systems…**its enormously complex**"
- **Cultural integration** — "**'Not Invented Here' syndrome** and other
  byproducts of two organizations as one"
- **Clear strategic alignment** — "driven by clear strategic objectives and a
  shared vision"
- **Effective leadership transition and post-merger integration** — **"this is
  where it usually falls apart……"**

## Choosing the structure — `SOURCE FACT`

| **CHOOSE A MERGER WHEN…** | **CHOOSE AN ACQUISITION WHEN…** |
|---|---|
| Two companies of similar size and strength | One company significantly larger or wealthier |
| Goal is a new identity and culture | **Speed is essential — hostile bids can move fast** |
| Both want equal footing in governance | Goal is to eliminate a competitor or gain technology |
| Combining patents, IP, or R&D pipelines is the priority | Acquirer wants full strategic control |
| *Industries: pharmaceuticals, banking, telecoms* | *Industries: tech, retail, media, energy* |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Diligence follows an exclusive LOI**, runs in a virtual data room, and
   spans financial, legal, operational, commercial, IT, and management workstreams.
2. **EV + WC = total needed to buy and run** — the purchase price isn't the cost.
3. **Cost synergies are bankable (12–24 months); revenue synergies are a promise
   (3–5 years); dis-synergies are always underestimated.**
4. **Five real diligence failures** from the instructor's career: Coral Ventures,
   Enron/Arthur Andersen, BTI/Fujitsu, Picolight (covenants), Occam (forecasts).
5. **"12 is the New 5"** — without cheap debt, PE now needs 12% EBITDA growth
   where 5% once sufficed.
6. **$3.7T of dry powder, 40% of it aged** — deployment, not fundraising, is the
   constraint.
7. **Secondaries are now mainstream**, giving employees and LPs liquidity
   pre-exit.
8. **Strategy → build, buy, or borrow.**
9. **Escrow is 8–15% of purchase price for 1–2 years; exclusivity is the only
   binding term in the term sheet.**
10. **HSR threshold $126.4M; 30-day waiting period; 2–5% of deals need remedies;
    Qualcomm paid NXP $2B when blocked.**
11. **M&A's hit rate looks like VC's, not PE's** — 3 of 4 deals fail to create
    shareholder value.
