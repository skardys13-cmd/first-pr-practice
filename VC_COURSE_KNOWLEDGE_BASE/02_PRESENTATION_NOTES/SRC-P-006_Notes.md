### The CVC pathway — Cisco / Cerent — `SOURCE FACT`

The deck lays out a three-step sequence:
> "Those firms may also be your **Go To Market partner first**…..
> Then they make an **investment**…..like **Cisco led a VC round in Cerent**…..
> Then they **buy the company**…..like **Cisco bought Cerent**"

**Why this matters** — this is the strategic logic of CVC in one example:
partner → invest → acquire. For a founder it means a CVC investor is
simultaneously a distribution channel and a likely exit. `CLAUDE INFERENCE`:
it also explains the higher valuation tolerance — the corporate is buying an
option on an acquisition it may want anyway.

### Working in CVC — `SOURCE FACT`, "Corporate Base and Benefits for those in VC Arm"

> - **"No reliance on fundraising! This is a huge reduction in workload"**
> - **"Predictable Bonuses and Incentives"**
> - **"Job Security since its not tied to raising funds"**
> - **"Less dependence on carried interest upside"**

Headed "Corporate VC — No funds has implications."

**Direct career relevance.** This is the course's explicit comparison of CVC
versus fund VC *as a job*, and it maps onto Seth's stated interest in corporate
venture capital. The trade is stability and no fundraising burden against
carried-interest upside. Cross-reference `SRC-P-001`'s "no sufficient returns —
no more funds; many venture firms are one fund and done," which is precisely the
risk a CVC role does not carry.

**The Financials 2 requirement is the notable one:** a revenue-vs-net-income
graph specifically to **show the valley of death**. This ties straight back to
`SRC-P-001`'s definition of VC as financing through that valley — the pitch is
expected to make the funding gap visible rather than hide it.

### Section-by-section guidance — `SOURCE FACT`

**Tagline** — "Example: FedEx: *When it absolutely, positively has to be there
overnight.*"

**Problem** — **"NAIL THIS CHART!"** · "WHAT PROBLEM ARE YOU SOLVING? …that
creates a HIGH GROWTH opportunity?"

**Business Model** — "How you make money on a per item or per subscriber basis
…….then take that to scale." And a distinction worth keeping:
**"NOTE: a Business Model is not a Financial Plan…but it feeds it."**

**Market Size** — benchmark given: **"Average GDP growth in US Last 20 years
~2%"**, against a "huge, fast growing market" example (weight management).
`CLAUDE INFERENCE`: the point is that a market growing at GDP is not a venture
market; growth must clear ~2% by a wide margin.

**Go-to-Market / Partnerships** — `PROFESSOR / COURSE VIEW`:
> "Note that the partner you seek…….are likely your best way to GO TO MARKET in
> some cases **AND…..your Partners are often the ones that are most likely to
> BUY YOUR COMPANY**"

Same insight as Cisco/Cerent, stated as general advice: **your GTM partners are
your acquirer pipeline.**

**Why Now?** — "Explain historical 'evolution' of your category, define any
recent trends that make your solution valuable." With the heuristic:
> **"Its pretty easy to see if something is right……its very hard to time your
> investment right"**

Dropbox's "Why Now" slide is used as the real-world example.

**Underlying Magic / IP** — "Do you have any Patents applied for? Anything most
unique about your approach?"

## The Parkiva worked example

A parking-marketplace company used as the running Series A example
(`[UNCLEAR]` whether this is a student project or an instructor construction).

`SOURCE FACT`:
- **Problem/market sizing:** "5% of US is public parking lots · 5%+ of US is
  private parking · **60% of US driveways are unused throughout the year**"
- **Solution:** "tap the massive unused parking areas with an **Uber-like app
  approach**"
- **Key differentiators:** daily parking rate, sell by space, seller-driven pricing
- **Market validation:** "Parkiva Trialed a Demo Product on **Homecoming Game
  2023**" — **90%** of users said they would use it again; **85%** said they
  would use it 2+ times a year
- **Seed stage:** **"$1M GRANT (so it's not dilutive)"**, plus a demo/survey
- **IP:** "Our Unique DPA Model — Patent Applied For"

**The non-dilutive grant point is worth keeping** — the deck flags grant funding
explicitly as capital that costs no ownership, in a course otherwise focused on
how expensive equity is (`SRC-P-005`).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Over half of all VC is corporate VC** — 3,000+ companies, 50 named S&P 500 arms.
2. **CVC pays higher valuations** because it buys strategic value, not just return.
3. **Partner → invest → acquire** is the CVC pathway (Cisco/Cerent), and your
   GTM partners are your most likely acquirers.
4. **A CVC job trades carry upside for no fundraising, predictable comp, and
   security** — the explicit fund-VC-vs-CVC career comparison.
5. **15 of 22 pitch sections are mandatory**, including a full financial
   spreadsheet and a revenue-vs-net-income graph showing the valley of death.
6. **A venture market must grow far faster than ~2% US GDP.**
7. **"Easy to see if something is right; very hard to time your investment right."**
