## The Three Ways to Put Money In

### 1. Preferred Shares — PRICED, via a term sheet
`SOURCE FACT`
- Sold at a **price per share established by the pre/post money valuation in the
  term sheet**
- Carry rights ("Protective Provisions") **above the COMMON** that founders and
  employees hold
- **Requires a company valuation to set the price**

### 2. Convertible Note (or venture debt) — UNPRICED
`SOURCE FACT`
- Company agrees to **"borrow" money temporarily**
- Buys an **option to convert to preferred shares at the future round price**
- **Until paid or converted it is debt, and is senior to preferred shares — so
  it is paid back first**
- **Does not require a valuation** — "there is no price yet…..until the next round"

### 3. SAFE Note — a warrant, not debt
`SOURCE FACT`
- **"A SAFE is a 'warrant'"** — an agreed future right to purchase equity later
- **It is NOT debt**
- Buys preferred **at some future round price, at a discount (~15%)**
- **Term can be indefinite**
- No valuation required
- **"Very Simple, Very Fast (why it is attractive)"**
- Simple · Agreement · For · Equity

**The organizing trade-off** — `CLAUDE INFERENCE`: all three are distinguished
along two axes the deck returns to repeatedly — *must you price the company
today?* and *where do you sit in the payout stack?* Preferred: priced, junior to
debt. Convertible note: unpriced, senior (it's debt). SAFE: unpriced, not debt,
compensated by a discount instead of seniority.

## Liquidation Preference

`SOURCE FACT` — **"Liquidation Preferences Stack Up….ahead of Common."**
**"Preferred Right to be paid back first."**
**"THESE PREFERENCES CUMULATE WITH ROUNDS IN ORDER."**

### Two types

**Non-participating** — "means the preferred don't 'participate' in more payout
after getting their 1x back. Then wait until it's better to convert to common."
Flagged as **"this is most common."**

**Participating** — preferred takes its preference *and* shares in the remainder.

### Variants named on the slides
- "Participating Preferred" — 1x LP
- "NON Participating Preferred" — 1x LP *(most common)*
- **"1x Participating Preferred with a 2x CAP"**
- **"3x Non Participating"**

### The Convert Point
`SOURCE FACT` — **"'Convert Point' is where Preferred are better off converting
to Common. That right to Convert is in the Term Sheet."**

The convert point is also what governs liquidation-preference payouts: the
investor takes whichever is larger — the preference, or their pro-rata share as
common.

### Conversion at IPO
`SOURCE FACT` — "Due to automatic conversion clauses, convertible preferred stock
**automatically converts into common stock during an IPO. The preferred
stockholders forego their liquidation preferences upon this conversion.**"

**Why this matters** — `CLAUDE INFERENCE`: liquidation preference is downside
protection that *disappears* in the best outcome. It shapes behavior in mediocre
exits, not great ones. This is a frequent interview follow-up.

## Anti-Dilution

`SOURCE FACT` — anti-dilution provisions "help protect investors' shares from
losing value in a **'down round'** (when the POST money valuation goes DOWN in a
new round)."

### Ratchets
| Type | Mechanics | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Full ratchet** | "price down 20%? …get 20% more shares as recompense" | **"Most stringent protection"** |
| **Weighted average ratchet** | "Price went down 20%, but you only get that times the ratio of new money to old" | **"The most common, less stringent protection"** |

## Other Term Sheet Provisions

`SOURCE FACT`, each in the course's own words:

- **Right of First Refusal** — "gives its holders 'first dibs' on any share sale
  in a startup."
- **Co-Sale / "Tag Along" Rights** — "a protective measure for minority
  shareholders that allows them to sell their shares when a majority shareholder
  sells their stake."
- **Registration Rights** — "'REGISTRATION' means you register the shares for
  sale with the SEC — this is part of the IPO Process… a mandatory step in an
  IPO. This right gives investors the power to require a company to register its
  shares with the SEC. **Means they can vote to make the company register for an
  IPO.**"
- **Pro Rata Rights** — "allows investors to maintain their ownership percentage
  when new shares are issued… the option to purchase additional shares in future
  funding rounds. **…..you can't kick them out of the syndicate (investor group)**"
- **Information Rights** — "Rights of investors to receive certain information…
  financial statements, business plans, and annual reports. **Important if you do
  not have a large enough investment to warrant a board seat — you will still get
  the board package. This is what many corporate venture arms want — they don't
  want to actively have board responsibilities, but they want to know everything
  about the company progress.**"

**The information-rights note is a direct link to `SRC-P-006` (Corporate VC)**
and is unusually practical: it explains the *mechanism* by which a CVC stays
informed without taking governance responsibility. Directly relevant to Seth's
stated CVC interest.

## THE CENTRAL EXERCISE — Two Rounds versus One Round

`SOURCE FACT` — "MULTI-ROUND DILUTION: Comparing taking money in rounds at
increasing price vs one round at initial price."

### Setup — NewCo raises $40M total, two ways

**Case 1 — $40M over Two Rounds**
```
Series A:  $12M valuation   →  raised $18M at that price
Series B:  $100M valuation  →  raised $22M at that price
```

**Case 2 — $40M over One Round**
```
Series A:  $12M valuation   →  raised ALL $40M at that price
```

### Cap table construction — `SOURCE FACT`

- **Par value at first: $0.01** — "basically unvalued." "Common for initial
  shares of a company to be set at a par value of $.01 per share… a nominal
  amount that allows founders to purchase large quantities of shares at minimal
  cost. May set it even lower, like $.001 or $.0001 per share."
- Then **add an Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP)** to the cap table
- Then Series A term sheet at **$12M pre-money**, sell $18M to the VC at Series A price
- Recompute post-A cap table
- Then Series B at **$100M pre-money**, sell $22M — "now $40M total"
- Recompute post-B cap table

`[VISUAL NOT CAPTURED]` — every cap table itself is a slide graphic. Only the
headings and the final results survive text extraction. **This is the single
biggest recoverable gap in the deck set** — the tables show the share counts and
ownership percentages behind the results below.

### The interim question — `SOURCE FACT`
> "NewCo has just received an offer to buy the company for **$38M**!
> How much will the founders and investors receive of this windfall/sale?"

This is a **liquidation-preference trap question**: $38M is *less* than the $40M
invested, so the preference stack consumes the entire proceeds and the founders'
common is worth approximately nothing. The deck poses it right after teaching
that preferences "cumulate with rounds in order." The stated answer is in a
`[VISUAL NOT CAPTURED]` table.

### The IPO and the results — `SOURCE FACT`

> "NOW GO IPO — SELL ('FLOAT') **18.1% more shares (750K shares)**"

| | **Case 1 — Two Rounds** | **Case 2 — One Round** |
|---|---|---|
| **Founders made** | **$16.9M each** | **$12.4M each** |
| **Investors made** | **$381M on $40M** | **$466M on $40M** |
| Investor multiple | **~9x** | **~12x** |

`SOURCE FACT` — the deck's own summary of Case 2: "FOUNDERS MADE $12.4M each..
**ABOUT 40% LESS** than A and B two rounds" and "INVESTORS MADE $466M on $40M
investment ..**from ~9x to ~12x**."

### The lesson

`PROFESSOR / COURSE VIEW` — **Raising in staged rounds at increasing valuations
is worth roughly 40% more to each founder.** Taking all the money up front at
the early, low valuation hands the extra ownership — and ~3x more multiple — to
the investors.

`CLAUDE INFERENCE` — the mechanism: in Case 1 only $18M is priced at the $12M
valuation; the remaining $22M is priced at $100M, buying far less of the company.
In Case 2 the entire $40M is priced at $12M pre-money. **The scarce resource is
not capital — it is the valuation at which you sell it.** This connects directly
to burn rate and runway: the reason to raise only what you need is that the next
dollar is cheaper if you can wait for it.

**This is the course's single best interview example.** It answers "explain
dilution," "why not raise all the money at once?", and "walk me through a cap
table" with concrete numbers and a counterintuitive result.

## Formulas / Calculations

- **Pre/post money sets the price per share** — "Preferred Shares are sold at a
  PRICE per share that is established by the Pre/Post Money valuation in the
  Term Sheet"
- **Full ratchet**: shares added ∝ the % price decline
- **Weighted average ratchet**: full-ratchet effect × (new money / old money)
- **Float at IPO**: 750K shares = 18.1% more shares
- Investor multiple: $381M/$40M ≈ 9x; $466M/$40M ≈ 12x

## Key Takeaways

1. **Three instruments, two questions:** must you price the company now, and
   where do you sit in the stack?
2. **Liquidation preference is downside insurance that vanishes at IPO** —
   automatic conversion forfeits it. It matters most in mediocre exits.
3. **Preferences cumulate by round, in order** — which is why a $38M offer
   against $40M raised can leave founders with nothing.
4. **Non-participating 1x is the market standard**; participating, capped
   participating, and 3x variants exist and are investor-favorable.
5. **Weighted average is the common anti-dilution ratchet; full ratchet is the
   punitive one.**
6. **Information rights are how corporate VCs stay close without taking a board
   seat.**
7. **Staging rounds is worth ~40% more to founders.** The headline result.
