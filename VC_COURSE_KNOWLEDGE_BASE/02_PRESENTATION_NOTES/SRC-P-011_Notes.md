## Platform / Add-On / Roll-Up Strategy

`SOURCE FACT` — **"this deal strategy is ~30% of all PE deals"**

### Definitions
- **Platform** — "The first acquisition that 'adds on' other complementary
  acquisitions"
- **Add-Ons** — "Complementary — enhanced technology or market opportunities for
  larger platform companies. **Typically retain their identity after the
  investment.** Ideally, acquired with strategic cross-selling benefit [to] both
  companies."

### Terminology variants — `SOURCE FACT`
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bolt-On** | = Add-On |
| **Tuck-In** | = Add-On, "but tend to refer to **tech** tuck-ins" |
| **Roll-Up** | = Add-On, "but tend to refer to a **cookie cutter model for success that is implemented across each roll-up for rapid scalability**" |

### Scale of the strategy — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Platform deals ~30% of all PE transactions**
- **Including add-on deals: over 60% of all deals**
- **In 2023, over 70% of PE-backed acquisitions by dollar value involved an
  add-on deal** — "demonstrating their dominance in the market"
- **97% of PE firms surveyed in 2024 anticipate at least one in four portfolio
  companies to undertake a bolt-on acquisition before exit**
- **Dry powder estimated at $2.62 trillion globally**

### Why PE firms pursue add-ons — `SOURCE FACT`
- "Smaller acquisitions can **most quickly multiply the value of already healthy
  businesses**"
- "Add-ons are **much less expensive and lower risk**"
- **"Add-ons attract far less LP scrutiny compared to platform investments"**
- "They can be quick….that matters in flipping companies yet doing multiple
  deals…… **as few as 2 months to close a deal…average is 12–18 months**"

> **"'Flip' the neighborhood ….not just the House"**

That line is the course's summary of the whole rollup thesis.

### Returns — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Platform + add-on: ~20% IRR versus industry ~15%**
- "Platform Deals typically yield higher returns but **require more time** to
  reach scale and profitability"
- "**Risk:** Platform deals involve higher risks, especially in the initial
  stages, but they create a stable foundation"
- "**Synergies:** Add-on acquisitions are usually more effective in generating
  cost synergies and revenue expansion"

### Worked firm example — Shore Capital Partners — `SOURCE FACT`
> "By 2023, Shore Capital had acquired **56 platform companies and ~800 add-on
> acquisitions** — **~15 deals each on average** as add-ons. The firm reported an
> **average gross cash-on-cash return of 7.0x, median of 5.5x on 14 exits,
> placing it in the top 1% of PE firms.**"

### Two types of consolidation — `SOURCE FACT`
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Serve multiple markets** | **General Electric** — aviation, energy, healthcare, home appliances |
| **Provide multiple services to one market** | **United Technologies** — "trying to be uber supplier to Boeing" |

### Market trends — `SOURCE FACT`
- **Consolidation in Fragmented Markets** — "Target fragmented industries (e.g.
  healthcare, technology, industrials) where add-ons can rapidly scale businesses"
- **Global Expansion** — "Use add-ons to enter new geographic markets"
- **Technological Integration** — "especially in industries where digital
  transformation can drive growth (e.g. software, fintech)"

## HOW TO ACTUALLY SELECT A PE DEAL — the course's screening process

`SOURCE FACT` — "PE DEAL SELECTION — EXAMPLE PROCESS". This is a repeatable
method, and the most directly usable framework in the deck:

**Step 1 — Pick a market space of interest**
- "With multiple players in the space"
- "Ideally with an incumbent or a highly valued one …..**THAT WILL BE YOUR
  REFERENCE for 'GOOD'**"
- "Your strategy may involve some trends in the space….do some research on what
  analysts are saying the future trends are"

**Step 2 — Study the space and the companies in it**
- "**FIND A REFERENCE POINT of a SOLID COMPANY IN THE SPACE. NOTE HOW MUCH IT
  TRADES FOR (EBITDA)** ….basically ….what do companies trade for in the space?"
- "**Use Market Cap/EBITDA……or EV/EBITDA**"
- "What's hot or could be in the space? Something to pursue"

**Step 3 — Look at the bottom of the list**
> "**PE MAY FOCUS ON BOTTOM PLAYERS…** GO TO **YAHOO FINANCE** AND START
> COMPARING THEIR INCOME STATEMENT to the TOP PLAYERS.
> **IS THERE EBITDA? ANYTHING STAND OUT AS BROKEN OR NOT SCALING?
> FIX EM. Or ROLL THEM UP.**"

### The worked screen — footwear — `SOURCE FACT`

| | **BIRK** (Birkenstock) — top player | **BIRD** (Allbirds) — bottom player |
|---|---|---|
| Market cap | **$7.4B** | **$0.037B** ($37.37M) |
| Revenue multiple | **3.4x revenue** | **0.24x revenue** ($37M / $160M TTM) |
| EBITDA multiple | **118x EBITDA (!)** | **NA x EBITDA** |

> "Valuation based on revenue multiple: $37M/$160M = **0.24x rev** versus
> **MARKET AVE = 0.55x**. **Opportunity to double market cap if valued as
> 'normal' in market space** — based on this metric."

## Deal Sourcing

### What LPs say — `SOURCE FACT`
> "Firms pitch **proprietary sourcing** as part of their 'edge' to LPs.
>
> **'We only invest in managers who don't rely solely on bankers'**
> — LP from **ILPA panel 2024**
>
> **Proprietary deal flow = returns + differentiation + fundraising advantage.**"

### Why banker-sourced deals return less — `SOURCE FACT`
> "**INVESTMENT BANKERS ARE THE SECOND MOST COMMON WAY TO SOURCE DEALS.**
> BUT……Which type of Deal Source leads to the highest return?
>
> **The NON Investment Banker sourced deals yield better returns for PE** —
> because Bankers create an **'auction'** by running a 'process' that enables
> more bids, and lessens [the] ability to find a deal that has a price that has
> not been **'bid up'**."

**This is a genuinely important sourcing insight** and directly answers the VC/PE
interview question "how would you source deals?" — the answer is *proprietary*,
and the reason is price, not effort.

### The "Teaser" — `SOURCE FACT`
"STARTING A 'PROCESS' — WITH A **'TEASER'**. A TEASER: **first step document,
deal summary, succinct.** Investment bankers' first step shopping a deal."

### Why bankers only do larger deals — `SOURCE FACT`
> "SMALLER DEALS HAVE A HARD TIME GETTING ENOUGH REVENUE FOR AN INVESTMENT BANK
> TO PROFITABLY PROVIDE SERVICE.
>
> **Example: Selling a company for $10M would pay a banker $700K** to staff a
> team and run a road show 'process'…..**usually need at least $1M in fees to do
> that profitably.**"

*(Consistent with `SRC-P-001`'s ~7% of deal value banker fee.)*

### Deal size categories — `SOURCE FACT`
| Segment | Size |
|---|---|
| Large Cap | **$1B+** |
| Upper Middle Market | **$500M – $1B** |
| Middle Market | **$100M – $500M** |
| Lower Middle Market | **$50M – $100M** |
| Small Cap | **< $50M** |

### "Bottom feeding" — bankruptcy sourcing — `SOURCE FACT`
"BANKRUPTCY STRATEGY MORE COMMON FOR SMALLER FIRMS." **"'DISTRESSED/SPECIAL
SITUATIONS' IS THE LARGEST PE SECTOR FOCUS FOR DOING BANKRUPTCY DEALS."**

Monitoring: "Tracking Chapter 11 filings and distressed companies for potential
acquisitions. Utilizing public records and legal databases."

**Free research resources the course provides** — `SOURCE FACT`, worth keeping
as a practical sourcing toolkit:
- **PACER** (pacer.uscourts.gov) — $0.10/page, **first $30 per quarter waived
  for all users**, fee exemptions for academic research, free terminals at many
  federal courthouses
- **U.S. Courts Bankruptcy Forms** (uscourts.gov/forms/bankruptcy-forms)
- **SEC EDGAR** (sec.gov/edgar.shtml) — "Form 8-K often contains initial
  bankruptcy announcements." Note: **"EDGAR IS ALSO WHERE ALL OF THE IPO
  DOCUMENTS AND QUARTERLY, ANNUAL, AND EVENT FILINGS ARE AVAILABLE"**
- **FDIC** (fdic.gov/buying/owned/) — failed bank assets
- **U.S. Marshals Service Asset Forfeiture Sales**
- **American Bankruptcy Institute** (abi.org)
- **Retail Dive's Bankruptcy Tracker**
- **UCLA-LoPucki Bankruptcy Research Database** (lopucki.law.ucla.edu) — "Free
  academic database of large public company bankruptcies"
- Bloomberg / WSJ bankruptcy sections via university subscriptions

## Divestitures and Spin-Offs

### Divestiture — `SOURCE FACT`
"Selling off a company's assets or divisions. Done for strategic, financial, or
regulatory reasons. **Can increase focus on core business. Often leads to
improved financial performance.**"

**Why companies divest:** focus on core business · **reduce debt or raise
capital** · comply with regulatory requirements · eliminate underperforming units

### Spin-Off — `SOURCE FACT`
- "A division becomes an **independent company**"
- "**Existing shareholders receive shares of new entity**"
- **"No cash exchange between parent and new company"**
- Example: **"PayPal was spun off to be an independent, publicly traded
  company……after being a part of eBay's corporate structure for several years"**

## CASE STUDY — Calix divests its cabinet business to Clearfield

`SOURCE FACT` — a **first-hand case from the instructor's own company**, where he
was SVP Corporate Strategic Development (`SRC-P-001`).

### The cash position — the reason for the deal
```
2010 IPO:      Raised $83M in cash
2018 Q1:       Low point of just $3.2M ….and still losing money

The apparent number:        $39.8M in cash
  ...but the $30M credit line was tapped out — and broke a covenant
     (the rule to maintain profitability)
  → $9.8M in actual cash
  → −$5.6M expected burn
  → Just $3.2M in actual cash BUFFER
```

> **"CASH….WELL, NOT AS MUCH AS MIGHT THINK….."**
>
> "**WE NEEDED EMERGENCY CASH — so what did we do? We DIVESTED …… THE LOWEST
> GROSS MARGIN PRODUCT LINE WE HAD** to Clearfield Communications."

### Why this case matters

`CLAUDE INFERENCE` — three lessons compressed into one real episode:
1. **Headline cash is not available cash.** $39.8M on the balance sheet was
   $3.2M of genuine buffer once a drawn revolver and a broken covenant were
   accounted for. This is the single best illustration in the course of reading
   a balance sheet skeptically.
2. **A covenant breach converts a credit line from an asset into a liability.**
   Ties directly to the maintenance-covenant discussion in `SRC-P-008`.
3. **Divestiture as a financing tool** — and the logic of *which* unit to sell:
   the **lowest gross margin product line**, consistent with `SRC-P-008`'s
   "margin covers up a lot of sins."

**Strong interview material** — it is specific, quantified, and comes from a
practitioner who was in the room.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Platform/add-on/rollup is ~30% of PE deals alone, and over 60% including
   add-ons** — the dominant value-creation strategy, returning ~20% IRR vs ~15%.
2. **"Flip the neighborhood, not just the house."**
3. **Add-ons are cheap, fast, low-risk, and attract less LP scrutiny.**
4. **Deal selection method:** pick a space → find the best-in-class reference
   multiple → screen the bottom of the list on Yahoo Finance → fix or roll up.
5. **Proprietary sourcing beats banker-sourced deals** because auctions bid up
   the price — and LPs explicitly select managers on this.
6. **Bankers need ~$1M in fees**, so sub-$15M deals don't get a process.
7. **Carried interest is taxed at 15–20% capital gains rates** — a structural
   subsidy alongside the interest deduction.
8. **$2.62T of global dry powder** is waiting to be deployed.
9. **Calix: $39.8M of "cash" was really $3.2M** — and the fix was divesting the
   lowest-margin line.
