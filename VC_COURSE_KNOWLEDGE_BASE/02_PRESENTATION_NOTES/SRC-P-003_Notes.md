## PRICED vs UNPRICED — the organizing distinction

`SOURCE FACT` (slide 22) — "PRICING ROUNDS of FINANCING":

| **PRICED** | **NOT PRICED….yet** |
|---|---|
| **Equity investments** | **Convertible notes** |
| Pre-money / Post-money | **SAFEs (Simple Agreements for Equity)** |

### Priced equity — `SOURCE FACT`
- "Preferred Shares are sold at a **PRICE per share established by the Pre
  Money**"
- "It buys **PREFERRED SHARES**"
- "It has **rights above the COMMON** shares the founders and employees own"
- "**It requires a COMPANY VALUATION to set PRICE**"

### Convertible note — `SOURCE FACT`
- Company "borrows" money temporarily
- Buys an **option to convert to preferred at the future round price**
- **"Until paid or converted, it is a debt, and is senior to Preferred Shares, so
  it is paid back first"**
- No valuation required
- **New detail not in `SRC-P-005`:** "**With typically no payments made.
  Dividends accrue…. but are also typically not paid.**"

### SAFE — `SOURCE FACT`
- "It's a **'warrant' (agreed right)** to purchase Equity at a later date"
- **"It [is] NOT a debt, just a warrant"** to buy preferred at the future round
  price "(or a **discounted** version)"
- **"Its term is indefinite"**
- No valuation required
- **"Very Simple, Very Fast (why it is attractive)"**

## WORKED VALUATION EXAMPLES

### Example 1 — a priced seed then a VC round — `SOURCE FACT`
```
FIRST SEED ROUND to ESTABLISH Co
  Initial ownership of founders — NO INVESTORS YET

SECOND ROUND with VC INVESTMENT
  Add a $1M investment in Equity from an INSTITUTIONAL INVESTOR (VC)
  Priced at $4M PRE money
  So $5M POST money is the COMPANY'S NEW VALUATION
```
`[VISUAL NOT CAPTURED]` — the cap tables behind these are graphics.

### Example 2 — solving backwards from a stake — `SOURCE FACT`
> "**'I will buy a 5% stake in your company for $200,000'**
> **$200,000 / .05 = $4,000,000 Valuation. POST MONEY**
> The FOUNDERS own **95%** of the company, the investor owns **5%**
> Though [the] investor get[s] **Preferred Stock**"

And the general rule, stated on slide 29:
> **"THE POST MONEY is also the amount financed, divided by the amount that
> bought"** — i.e. `Post = Investment ÷ Ownership %`

**These two examples are the cleanest arithmetic in the whole deck set** for
"explain pre vs post money," and they run the calculation in both directions:
forward (pre + money = post) and backward (money ÷ % = post).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Priced vs unpriced is the organizing distinction** — equity is priced;
   convertible notes and SAFEs defer the price to the next round.
2. **Post = Investment ÷ Ownership%**, and **Pre + Money = Post.** Two worked
   examples run it both directions.
3. **Convertible note dividends accrue but are typically unpaid** — a detail not
   in `SRC-P-005`.
4. **Companies are re-valued "very arbitrarily" every round**, with ownership and
   board/management often changing with it.
5. **~90% are sold before IPO** — repeated from `SRC-P-002`.
6. **Thirty Iowa IPOs**, concentrated in financial services and insurance.
