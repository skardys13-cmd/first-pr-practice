The plan

Weighted to Path 1, the operations ladder, with the data and automation work kept as the thing that separates you rather than as a second career. Four phases of 91 tasks. A block is a week’s worth of work, not a calendar week — you reach block 12 when you have finished 77 tasks, whenever that happens. The whole thing is about 400 hours, so at your six to eight hours a week it runs roughly twelve months. The dashboard tracks the rate you actually keep.

The thing that actually gets you hired

Portfolio vault

Certifications prove you studied. A portfolio proves you operate. Every item here is a file with a link, a one-line result and a number — tick it when it exists and someone outside your head could open it. Starting this in month one is the whole advantage: you are documenting each process the week you are taught it, which means by month twelve you will own the documentation for a job you were still learning in January.

Compliance guardrail, read once and never forget it: nothing in this portfolio contains real client data. No names, no account numbers, no balances, no statements, no screenshots of Schwab Advisor Center or Redtail with live records. You build every artifact on synthetic data you generate yourself, and you clear anything public with your firm's compliance contact first. A single PII leak ends this plan and your career in the industry — and reviewers are more impressed by a clean synthetic dataset than by a redacted real one.

The number is the point

      
Compensation model

      
You asked for $80k+ on the move, $100k at year two and $130–150k at years four to six. From one month into your first RIA seat, those numbers are right but the years are not — operations titles track years of experience closely, and nobody moves from month 13 into an Operations Manager band. The re-based version below reaches the same destination roughly two to three years later, and the portfolio is what stops it taking longer than that.
