# Build me "Handoff" — a 100-day consultant transition tracker

Build a single-file HTML artifact and publish it. Read this whole brief first.

---

## Who this is for

I am a paraplanner, 30 days into my first seat at a **six-person RIA**. I am on
a **Mac**. I am studying for the **Series 65** on my own (outside any tracker —
do not build study tools).

**The situation:** our operations consultant leaves in roughly **100 days**. She
owns a large amount of recurring operational work that is not documented
anywhere. The firm is small and fluid, and open to people doing what they are
good at.

**What I am going to do:** learn her work, document it, take it over in stages,
and at Month 5 pitch a 90-day operations pilot. My goal is Director of
Operations or a hybrid operations role within about six months. Longer term I
want to be COO of an RIA.

**My time:** 2–4 protected hours during the work week for this. My evenings are
3–4 hours and they belong to a different plan (see "Do not duplicate" below).

## The three source documents

I already wrote these. The artifact must operationalise them, not restate them.

### Document 1 — Conversation Playbook

The core message I lead with: *I am not asking the firm to gamble on a new
position. I am offering to preserve the consultant's knowledge, take on work in
stages, and prove the value before we formalize anything.*

**What must be convincing:** business need (consultant leaves in 3–4 months,
creating a knowledge and ownership gap) · low risk (learn and assume work in
stages while still meeting current responsibilities) · partnership (I want her
help documenting and transferring, not to push her out) · proof first (judged on
handoff coverage, task reliability, documented workflows, time returned to
advisors) · clear boundaries (compliance decisions stay with the CCO; I
coordinate workflows, evidence, deadlines and follow-through) · long-term value
(one accountable internal owner reduces dropped tasks, consultant dependence,
and advisor time spent managing the business).

**Conversation 1 — the consultant.** A spoken script, a short email/Teams
version, and seven questions that show maturity:
- What recurring work do you own daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annually?
- What usually breaks, gets delayed, or requires follow-up?
- Which responsibilities require judgment versus a checklist?
- Which deadlines or controls are highest risk if missed?
- Which vendors, systems and people do you rely on?
- What should I learn first, and what should I not touch yet?
- What would make you comfortable saying I can own a process unsupervised?

**Conversation 2 — the manager.** Ask for permission to build the handoff, not
for a title. The six specific approvals to request:
1. A 30-minute kickoff with the manager, consultant and me
2. One 30-minute handoff session with the consultant each week for 12–16 weeks
3. Permission to document workflows in an approved shared location
4. Two or three low-risk processes I can begin owning once trained
5. A 15-minute monthly progress review with the manager
6. A decision meeting at the end of Month 5 based on agreed evidence

**Month-5 pitch.** Ask for a **90-day Operations Coordinator or Operations
Manager pilot** — not an open-ended promotion. Bring: one-page result summary
(before → after), responsibility map (owned / backup / approval owner /
cadence), SOP index (completed, tested, still missing), scorecard (on-time
tasks, aging issues, time saved, errors/rework), and a 90-day role charter
(scope, boundaries, goals, review date).

**Language to avoid → use instead:**
| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| "I deserve a promotion after six months." | "I want the role judged by business need and results." |
| "I can replace the consultant." | "I want to preserve her knowledge and earn ownership gradually." |
| "I'll run compliance." | "I'll coordinate the operational workflow under the designated compliance owner." |
| "I can automate everything." | "I'll simplify, document, and automate only where approved and controlled." |
| "Operations is what I really want." | "This is a gap I can help the firm solve." |

Tone: curious, useful, specific. Ask to learn and prove — not to be promised a title.

### Document 2 — Five-Month Execution Plan (20 weeks)

**End state:** by the consultant's exit, every transferred responsibility has a
visible owner, backup, schedule and written procedure. By end of Month 5,
leadership has enough evidence to approve or reject a formal operations pilot.

**Operating rules (these are non-negotiable and must be visible in the artifact):**
- Do not let this hurt current paraplanner performance; agree available hours with the manager
- Do not redesign a process until the current process, owner, risk and approval requirements are understood
- Store no client data in personal files; approved firm systems and access controls only
- Compliance ownership stays with the designated CCO/qualified person
- Baseline first, then confirm realistic targets with leadership
- Do not fake the numbers. If a target cannot be measured cleanly, label it estimated and show the method

**Month 1 — Map the firm and the consultant's work**
- W1: secure manager approval, schedule weekly handoff time, agree where documents live
- W1: ask the consultant to list every daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly/annual/event-driven responsibility
- W2: build the responsibility map — task, cadence, system, owner, backup, approver, deadline, risk if missed
- W2: shadow one complete client cycle — prospect/onboarding through meeting prep, implementation, service
- W3: map five core workflows, recording every handoff, duplicate entry, delay, unclear owner, advisor interruption
- W4: rank issues by risk, hours consumed, frequency, ease of improvement; review the map with the consultant

*Targets:* 100% of known recurring responsibilities inventoried · 5+ end-to-end
workflow maps · 10+ friction points with top 5 ranked · baseline for 4+ KPIs ·
4 handoff/shadow sessions completed.
*Deliverables:* responsibility inventory v1 · five workflow maps · risk and
bottleneck list · baseline scorecard · open-questions list.

**Month 2 — Document and test the work**
- W5: select the eight highest-frequency or highest-risk processes
- W5–7: write each SOP while observing the work — trigger, inputs, steps, owner, approvals, evidence, deadline, exception path
- Each week: run one SOP yourself in a safe setting or as a tabletop walkthrough; have the consultant correct it
- W7: create the master recurring calendar with reminders and backups
- W8: select two low-risk recurring processes for formal ownership and get manager approval

*Targets:* 8+ usable SOPs, 100% consultant-validated · 4+ tested live or by
tabletop · one complete recurring calendar · primary and backup named for 90%+
of recurring responsibilities · 2–3 processes selected for Month 3.
*Deliverables:* SOP library v1 · recurring calendar · ownership and backup
matrix · approved Month 3 transfer list.

**Month 3 — Own selected processes and measure reliability**
- W9: take full ownership of the first two approved processes; consultant observes rather than leads
- W10: add a third if quality and current-role performance hold
- Every Monday: publish a 15-minute operations list — priority, owner, due date, blocker
- Every Friday: close completed items, escalate aging items, log time saved, errors, lessons
- W11–12: fix one approved bottleneck using the simplest change possible; compare before and after

*Targets:* own 3 recurring processes end to end · 95%+ of owned recurring tasks
on time · zero material client/compliance misses · one cycle time or touch count
down 20%+ · 2+ hours/week of advisor capacity returned.
*Deliverables:* weekly operations list · issue/escalation log · first
before-and-after improvement · monthly result summary.

**Month 4 — Complete the consultant handoff**
- W13: compare the responsibility inventory against what has actually transferred; highlight every gap
- W13–14: perform transferred work with the consultant available only for exceptions
- W14: run a deadline simulation for the next 90 days — monthly, quarterly, vendor, billing, reporting, compliance-coordination dates
- W15: have a second team member follow three SOPs with no verbal help; correct unclear steps
- W16: get written sign-off from consultant and manager on owned, backup and retained responsibilities

*Targets:* 80–90% of in-scope recurring operational work transferred (or
explicitly assigned elsewhere) · 12–15 validated SOPs · owner and backup on 100%
of in-scope recurring work · 95%+ on-time · no high-priority item overdue more
than 2 business days without documented escalation.
*Deliverables:* final handoff matrix · 90-day operating calendar · backup test
results · consultant transition sign-off.

**Month 5 — Prove the value and present the role**
- W17: operate without routine consultant support; record every exception, interruption and failure point
- W18: finish the final two or three SOPs and a simple new-hire/backup guide
- W19: calculate results from the baseline — hours saved, turnaround, on-time rate, issue aging, rework, coverage
- W19: draft the 90-day role charter — scope, boundaries, scorecard, manager, title, review date
- W20: present the result summary and request a formal 90-day pilot

*Targets:* 15–20 validated core SOPs · own 5–7 recurring processes · 95%+
on-time for two consecutive months · 4–6 advisor hours/week returned, supported
by logs or before/after timing · repeat issues or rework down 25%+ from
baseline · a documented decision: pilot approved, revised, or declined with
specific next requirements.
*Deliverables:* one-page impact report · operations scorecard · final SOP index
· 90-day pilot charter · decision meeting notes.

**The five-month scorecard** (baseline → M1 → M2 → M3 → M4 → M5 target):
| Measure | Base | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recurring responsibilities inventoried | — | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| Validated SOPs | 0 | 0–2 | 8 | 10–12 | 12–15 | 15–20 |
| Processes personally owned | 0 | 0 | 0–1 | 3 | 4–6 | 5–7 |
| Owned tasks completed on time | base | base | ≥95% | ≥95% | ≥95% | ≥95% |
| Advisor/team hours returned weekly | 0 | measure | 0–1 | ≥2 | 3–5 | 4–6 |
| Repeat issues / rework | base | base | — | −10% | −20% | −25% |

**Weekly rhythm — about 2–4 protected hours.** This is the heartbeat of the
whole thing and the artifact should be built around it:
| When | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Monday, 15 min | Set top priorities, owners, due dates, blockers | Weekly ops list |
| Handoff session, 30 min | Learn one process, review last week's execution | Updated SOP + questions |
| Build block, 60–120 min | Document, test or improve one item | Completed deliverable |
| Friday, 15 min | Update scorecard, escalate aging items | Impact log entry |
| Monthly, 15 min | Show manager results, risks, next transfers | Decision/approval notes |

### Document 3 — Command Center templates

These are currently Word tables. **I want four of them to become live, working
tools inside the artifact**, not text I retype elsewhere:

**1. Master responsibility register** — responsibility · cadence · primary ·
backup · approver · system · risk/deadline · link to its SOP.
*Completion rule: every recurring responsibility must have all of those before
it counts as transferred.*

**2. Consultant handoff tracker** — item · observed · documented · tested ·
owned · signed off · open question. Five checkbox states per item, so I can see
at a glance how far each responsibility has moved. This is the single most
important view in the app.

**3. SOP index + SOP template** — index: SOP · owner · backup · version/date ·
tested by · status · link. Template fields: process name · purpose (what
outcome does this create) · trigger and deadline · owner/backup/approver ·
systems and permissions · inputs (what must exist before starting) · steps
(numbered, one action each, include exact handoffs) · controls/evidence (what
proves it was done correctly) · exceptions/escalation (what can go wrong, who
decides) · output and storage · tested by/date · next review date.
*Writing an SOP should be filling in a form, not facing a blank page.*

**4. KPI scorecard + impact log** — scorecard rows: on-time recurring tasks
(completed by deadline ÷ due, target ≥95%) · high-priority aging (items overdue
>2 business days, target 0 un-escalated) · process cycle time (target −20%) ·
rework/repeat issues (target −25%) · advisor hours returned (target 4–6/wk) ·
SOP coverage (validated ÷ in-scope core processes, 90–100%) · backup coverage
(responsibilities with trained backup ÷ total, 100%). Each with baseline,
current, target, source, owner.
Impact log: date · problem/baseline · action · result · evidence · hours saved
per week.

**5. Questions log + decision log** — questions: date · question · asked of ·
answer/source · follow-up. Decisions: date · decision/approval · decision owner
· reason · next review. These two protect me when memory gets fuzzy and they
are what I bring to the Month 5 meeting.

Also worth building in, from the same document: the **weekly command center**
(week of · top outcome · manager review date · priorities table with
owner/due/status/blocker · closeout: completed / missed / escalations / lesson
for next week), the **issues and improvement backlog** (issue · frequency ·
risk · time cost · effort · priority · next action), the **systems and vendor
inventory** (system · purpose · business owner · admin · renewal · cost ·
integration issue — and never store passwords in it), and the **monthly manager
update** (what I took ownership of · what improved, with one or two
before-and-after measures · current risks with impact and owner · decisions
needed · next month's targets).

## What to build

A single self-contained HTML file, published as an Artifact. Working name:
**Handoff**. Change it if you have something better, but keep it short and
specific — it sits next to two other launchers on my desktop.

### Structure

Unlike my other tracker, **this one IS calendar-driven** — the consultant
leaves on a real date. Anchor everything to two dates I set: her **exit date**
and the **Month 5 decision date**. Show days remaining prominently and
permanently.

Twenty weeks, W1–W20, mapped from the Month 1–5 actions above. Each week's
actions become tickable tasks with the same interaction model I already like:

- Every task can be **part-completed** (quarter buttons: ¼ ½ ¾ Done) and the
  partial minutes count toward hours logged
- Every task can be **pushed** to later without becoming "overdue" — but
  because this plan has a real deadline, pushing something should show how much
  slack is left before the consultant's exit, not just move it
- Every task **opens a workspace**: how to do it (numbered, tickable steps), a
  place to actually do the work (a list builder for list-type tasks, a notes
  area otherwise), and a link field
- Nothing goes red for being late. Show the consequence instead: "this was
  meant to be done by W6 and she leaves in 34 days"

### Tabs

1. **This week** — the Monday/handoff/build/Friday rhythm, what is due, what is
   carried, the countdown
2. **The 20 weeks** — the full plan by month, expandable
3. **Responsibility register** — live table, with the completion rule enforced
   visually (incomplete rows flagged)
4. **Handoff tracker** — the five-state progress view per item. Make this
   excellent; it is the thing I will look at most
5. **SOPs** — index plus the template as a fill-in form
6. **Scorecard** — the seven KPIs with baseline/current/target, plus the
   five-month scorecard table, plus charts
7. **Impact log** — what I fixed, with evidence and hours saved
8. **Questions & decisions** — the two logs
9. **Conversations** — the scripts from Document 1, copy-ready, plus the
   approvals checklist and the language-to-avoid table
10. **The Month 5 pitch** — assembles itself from everything above: the
    one-page result summary, the numbers, the charter. This should feel like
    the payoff of the whole app
11. **Backlog & systems** — the issues backlog and the vendor inventory
12. **Operating manual** — the rules, the dates, settings

### Charts

At minimum: SOPs validated over time against the 15–20 target · responsibilities
by handoff state (observed/documented/tested/owned/signed-off) as a stacked
progress view · on-time rate trend · hours returned per week trend. Load the
`dataviz` skill before writing any chart code. Draw them as inline SVG, no
chart library.

### Design

Load the `artifact-design` skill first. This should feel like a sibling of my
other tracker, not a stranger: considered typography, a real palette, both
light and dark themes done properly through CSS custom properties on `:root`,
no emoji section markers, no gradient hero. It is a working tool that gets
opened on a Tuesday morning, not a landing page. Information density over
decoration.

### Technical

- Load the `artifact-capabilities` skill. Declare `{db:{}, downloads:true, sample:{}}`
- State persists to `localStorage` immediately and syncs via `db` when granted.
  Show me plainly which one is active and when I last exported
- **Export backup** must include everything — every register row, SOP, log
  entry, note and link
- The "how do I do this" steps for a task: hand-write them for the first two
  weeks so it works on day one, and use `sample` for the rest on demand
- Wrap every storage access in try/catch; the page must survive blocked
  localStorage without a single JS error
- Write a Playwright test suite alongside it and actually run it. Cover: fresh
  load, every tab rendering, tick/partial/push, the register and tracker
  round-tripping through storage, export/import, all-done state, and blocked
  localStorage

### Compliance — put this where I cannot miss it

This artifact holds **process metadata, not client data**. No client names,
account numbers, balances, statements, or screenshots of live records. Ever.
Compliance ownership stays with the firm's designated CCO — I coordinate
workflows, evidence and deadlines. The systems inventory records who controls
access, never credentials. Say this plainly on first load and on the register
and SOP tabs.

### One more thing

Give me a macOS `.command` launcher file for it, same as my others: a
double-clickable script that opens the published artifact and closes its own
Terminal window. I keep those on my desktop.
